                                             Fast Exact Booleans for Iterated CSG using Octree-Embedded BSPs

                                                                   Julius Nehring-Wirxela , Philip Trettnera , Leif Kobbelta
                                                         a RWTH Aachen, Lehrstuhl für Informatik 8, Ahornstraße 55, 52074 Aachen, Germany




                                        Abstract
                                        We present octree-embedded BSPs, a volumetric mesh data structure suited for performing a sequence
                                        of Boolean operations (iterated CSG) efficiently. At its core, our data structure leverages a plane-based




arXiv:2103.02486v1 [cs.CG] 3 Mar 2021
                                        geometry representation and integer arithmetics to guarantee unconditionally robust operations. These
                                        typically present considerable performance challenges which we overcome by using custom-tailored fixed-
                                        precision operations and an efficient algorithm for cutting a convex mesh against a plane. Consequently,
                                        BSP Booleans and mesh extraction are formulated in terms of mesh cutting. The octree is used as a global
                                        acceleration structure to keep modifications local and bound the BSP complexity. With our optimizations,
                                        we can perform up to 2.5 million mesh-plane cuts per second on a single core, which creates roughly 40–50
                                        million output BSP nodes for CSG. We demonstrate our system in two iterated CSG settings: sweep volumes
                                        and a milling simulation.
                                        Keywords: Plane-based Geometry, CSG, Mesh Booleans, BSP, Octree, Integer Arithmetic

can be used in our system. While our 256 bit arith-
metic presented in Section 3.2 provides nanometer
resolution and works for all practical meshes we
tested, this is technically a limitation. Replacing
our custom-tailored fixed-precision arithmetic with
gmp is always possible but causes at least a 10×
slowdown. A potential future avenue is to track
the precision requirements not per elementary op-
eration (as arbitrary-precision libraries do) but per
BSP plane and branch into different fixed-precision                               (c) Result of the milling simulation.
routines when constructing or classifying vertices.
This will not only remove any precision limitation
but might also provide a further speedup when op-
erations can be performed with less bit depth.
