Sato combined convex tracking at a distance with
Quinlan’s concave algorithm for near contact to create
a general collision detection system [15]. He stored the
closest pair of triangles for a number of time steps and
used the distance between that pair as a way to quickly
approximate the minimum distance.
Snyder used time coherence to track penetration depth
on spline models in a modeling system [16].
Numerical methods quickly updated previous points to
find new penetration depths. In [12] Lin used local
updates on global numerical methods to exploit the
temporal coherence of moving curved surfaces. These
methods depend on the coherence of the underlying
geometry for success.
