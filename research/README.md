# Profile the fillets, then certify the geometry

The literature does not hold a single algorithm that closes cadrs_kernel's measured gaps. It holds a set of well-documented production techniques for the slow paths and a newer body of *certification* methods that would make the fast paths provably complete. The gaps (fillets 9.2x/10.3x, sweeps 2.4x, drafts 1.9x, STEP import 1.9x and mass properties 11x slower than OpenCASCADE 7.8, per the [kernel README](../crates/cadrs_kernel/README.md)) have mostly engineering causes. OCCT's fixed Gauss rules use fewer points than cadrs. Its draft handles only planes, cylinders and cones without topology change. Its STEP reader skips most p-curve and SameParameter work, and its fillet walker follows tuned step rules that are fully readable in its source. Nobody has profiled where cadrs' fillet time goes, so the first work item is a per-phase profile of `fillet`/`fillet_geom`, not a new paper. After that, the strongest adoptions are these. In `mass`, exact degree-matched Gauss rules on untrimmed knot-span cells plus Gunderman-style boundary quadrature on trimmed cells. In `local`, an analytic fast path for drafts. In `fillet`, OCCT's cliff-edge blend functions and HP's trimming-path topology update, which retire the `blend` tool-body fallback. In `blend_corner`, a setback rule plus a Generalized Coons corner patch. In `ssi`, characteristic-point seeding (turning points and a Gauss-map no-loop test), so the signed-distance contour cannot miss a loop. In `boolean`, Jackson/OCCT-style local re-intersection, which replaces the global 1e-5/1e-4 retry. Exact arithmetic for curved geometry, GPU booleans, plane-based BSP booleans and ML methods are not worth adopting. Much of the classical journal literature could not be read in full (paywalls, bot checks). Several key algorithm descriptions, Grandine–Klein, Forsyth, Gonzalez-Ochoa and Hu–Wallner among them, rest on abstracts, citing papers or a researcher's recollection, and are flagged as such below.

## The measured gaps trace to point counts, restrictions and missing profiles, not missing theory

Each slow category in the README's OCCT comparison has an identifiable OCCT behaviour behind it, and in most cases that behaviour is a restriction or a shortcut rather than a better algorithm. For **mass properties**, OCCT's non-adaptive `BRepGProp` path is 11x faster than cadrs, but it is **1e-3 off on the blade hubs**, while cadrs runs at **0.52 of OCCT's adaptive integration to 1e-9** ([README](../crates/cadrs_kernel/README.md); [OCCT BRepGProp](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/refman/html/class_b_rep_g_prop.html)). Both systems use the same Timmer–Stern divergence-plus-Green's-theorem structure, so the gap is a point-count gap. The researcher's account of which Gauss orders OCCT picks comes from memory of the source and is unverified. For **drafts**, OCCT's `BRepOffsetAPI_DraftAngle` only "treats planar, cylindrical and conical faces" and tells callers "do not use shapes, which with a draft angle added to a face would modify the topology" ([OCCT DraftAngle](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/refman/html/class_b_rep_offset_a_p_i___draft_angle.html)). cadrs runs SMLib's general local operation for every draft. For **sweeps**, OCCT's default `Transformed` corner mode "assumes building a self-intersected shell", and its pipe surface is one bounded-degree approximation ([OCCT MakePipeShell header](https://eic-code-browser.sdcc.bnl.gov/lxr/source/include/opencascade/BRepOffsetAPI_MakePipeShell.hxx)). cadrs builds exact rails and validates corners. The offsets researcher's conclusion that this explains the 2.4x is an inference. For **STEP import**, OCCT always drops and recomputes p-curves on planes, leaves `read.stdsameparameter.mode` off and runs `ShapeFix` with "only if needed" modes ([OCCT STEP guide](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/overview/html/occt_user_guides__step.html); [XSTEPResource/STEP](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/V7_8_0/src/XSTEPResource/STEP)). Fast Part 21 tokenizers run at hundreds of MB/s ([openbim-step release notes](https://github.com/openbimrs/step/releases)), so the gap is most likely in `xbuild` model building, not in `step` lexing.

**Fillets** are the one gap with no published explanation. "No paper reports fillet timings" in any kernel. OCCT's cost structure is a start-solution search of at least 10 Newton attempts, a 4×4 Newton per trial step (30 iterations max), boundary re-framing solves, then a B-spline sweep approximation of degree ≤ 11 with up to 50 segments, corner plates, and two SameParameter passes ([Blend_Walking_4.gxx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/V7_8_0/src/Blend/Blend_Walking_4.gxx); [ChFi3d_Builder_2.cxx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/V7_8_0/src/ChFi3d/ChFi3d_Builder_2.cxx)). cadrs keeps a procedural contact-curve strip and skips the sweep approximation, so it should be able to beat OCCT on the geometry phase. The 9–10x gap therefore probably sits in contact-curve tracing and fitting, in the imprint and loop rebuild, or in the tool-body fallback through `blend`. The blends and intersection researchers point at different suspects (see the disagreements section), so a profile split by phase (start, march, fit, imprint, corner, fallback) has to come before any algorithm change. One more caveat applies to every ratio: OCCT 8.0 (May 2026) devirtualised elementary evaluation, stores B-spline arrays by value and added `BRepGProp` caching ([OCCT 8.0 release](https://github.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/releases/tag/V8_0_0)). Because `kernel_perf.py` benchmarks against 7.8, the baseline needs re-running against 8.0.1 before anyone quotes it.

## Fillets need OCCT's walker discipline and HP's topology bookkeeping, not new surfaces

The only fully readable production fillet is OCCT's ChFi3d/BRepBlend source, and its design is the de-facto standard. It solves a 4-unknown system (u1, v1, u2, v2) by Newton on section planes normal to a guide. The initial step is **5% of the guide length**. Steps are halved on rejection and grown ×1.5 up to that cap. A step is rejected when the chord makes more than about 11.5° (arccos 0.98) with the tangents, or when the sagitta estimate |Δt|²·|chord|/64 exceeds fleche² with **fleche = 5% of the largest radius**. When a contact point leaves its face, an inverse function is re-solved on the restriction arc, so the walk ends exactly on the boundary ([BlendFunc_ConstRad.cxx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/V7_8_0/src/BlendFunc/BlendFunc_ConstRad.cxx); [Blend_Walking_2.gxx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/V7_8_0/src/Blend/Blend_Walking_2.gxx); [Blend_Walking_3.gxx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/V7_8_0/src/Blend/Blend_Walking_3.gxx)). These constants are the sanity reference for `fillet_geom`'s contact-curve tracing: if cadrs takes many more sections per guide than this, step control is the payoff. OCCT also has **surface/restriction and restriction/restriction blend functions** (`SurfRstConstRad`, `RstRstConstRad`), which walk with one or both contact points pinned to an edge. This "cliff edge" case is how OCCT continues a fillet that would roll over a sharp edge ([ChFi3d_FilBuilder.cxx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/V7_8_0/src/ChFi3d/ChFi3d_FilBuilder.cxx)). Parasolid-based products expose the same case as "Retain cliff edges", tried alongside smooth overflow and notch ([T-FLEX help](https://tflex.com/help/eng/T-FLEX%20CAD/17/blendedges_overflowprocessing.htm)). It is the cheapest single addition that keeps overflow cases inside `fillet`'s local trimming instead of falling back to a subtracted tool body in `blend`.

The topology side has a clearer published recipe than the geometry. HP's two expired patents describe how a blend face replaces an edge when neighbouring faces must shrink or grow. Freitag removes edges with kill-edge-vertex until the blend boundary crosses them and kills faces with no crossing ([US5615317](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5615317)). Opitz handles *growing* faces by computing a trimming path from one blend boundary to the other through a depth-first search over half-curves of face/face intersections. Candidates are ordered by angle and then by length, and self-intersecting or edge-crossing paths are rejected. **"The complete path is computed before any topology is changed"** because topological edits "are generally not reversible in a unique way" ([US6133922](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6133922)). This is the general form of cadrs' "faces the strip hangs over grow to its cut" and "end faces that meet past the vertex grow over the fillet's end". It is the documented way to move more cases from `blend` into `fillet`.

At vertices, OCCT and the Várady literature agree on a ladder of methods: exact torus or sphere, then a fillet walked along a circular guide, then a transfinite fill, then a plate. OCCT's final rung, `GeomPlate`, warns in its own source that G1 constraints can cause "unpredicable undulations" ([ChFi3d_Builder_CnCrn.cxx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/V7_8_0/src/ChFi3d/ChFi3d_Builder_CnCrn.cxx)). OCCT also cuts stripes back by removing SurfData, not by a computed distance. The Geomagic/Várady patent gives the rule cadrs' `blend_corner` lacks. The **setback is computed from the widths of the neighbouring blends**, with a positive correction for tangent or cusp edges. The hole is bounded by alternating profile curves (sections of the edge blends) and spring curves (curves on the primary faces), up to 2n sides, with zero-length sides allowed ([US8004517](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8004517)). Because every boundary curve lies on an edge blend or a primary face, each corner edge has a p-curve on both of its faces by construction. The open Salvi–Várady papers then fill such a hole deterministically and non-iteratively, with G1 to the boundary ribbons by construction: a Generalized Coons patch, a composite ribbon patch, or an S-patch ([Salvi, Várady & Rockwood 2012](papers/blends/Salvi-Varady-Rockwood-2012-new-schemes-multi-sided-transfinite-interpolation.md); [Salvi 2019](papers/blends/Salvi-2019-G1-hole-filling-with-S-patches.md); [Salvi 2020](papers/blends/Salvi-2020-multi-sided-generalization-C0-Coons-patch.md)). The catch is exchange: these patches are not tensor-product NURBS, so writing them to STEP needs a conversion step (Salvi, Várady & Rockwood, CAD&A 18(1), 2021, [DOI 10.14733/cadaps.2021.156-169](https://doi.org/10.14733/cadaps.2021.156-169)).

For variable radius, conic and G2 sections the published work changes the *section generator*, not the tracing. OCCT runs the same walker with `BlendFunc_EvolRad` and a radius law ([ChFi3d_FilBuilder.cxx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/V7_8_0/src/ChFi3d/ChFi3d_FilBuilder.cxx)). Boeing's G2 fillet uses a clothoid–arc–clothoid section and moves the contact curves out to a larger "cut radius" ([US7518609](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7518609)), and the Pegna–Wolter linkage-curve theorem is the G2 acceptance check (via [Filkins 1991](https://archive.org/download/algorithmsforble00filk/algorithmsforble00filk.pdf)). Dupin cyclides are the only known closed-form variable-radius blends between natural quadrics ([Chandru, Dutta & Hoffmann 1989](papers/blends/Chandru-Dutta-Hoffmann-1989-variable-radius-blending-Dupin-cyclides.md)). They are worth knowing, but they do not address the measured gap. OCCT's exact cases stop at plane/plane, plane/cylinder and plane/cone; **cylinder/cylinder throws "particular case not written"** and is walked numerically ([ChFiKPart_ComputeData.cxx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/V7_8_0/src/ChFiKPart/ChFiKPart_ComputeData.cxx)). cadrs' exact prism and revolution cases already go further.

## Intersection gains come from finding every branch first, then tracing cheaply

cadrs' analytic dispatch in `ssi` already exceeds OCCT's. OCCT treats a torus analytically only when it is coaxial with or aligned to the other surface, and it walks every other pair ([IntPatch_Intersection.cxx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/master/src/ModelingAlgorithms/TKGeomAlgo/IntPatch/IntPatch_Intersection.cxx)). The weak point the literature identifies is completeness of the numeric path. A uv-grid contour of a signed distance is a lattice method, and the hyperbook's verdict on lattice methods is that grid resolution "can cause the method to miss important features … small loops and isolated points, which arise near or at tangency" ([Patrikalakis–Maekawa node107](https://web.mit.edu/hyperbook/Patrikalakis-Maekawa-Cho/node107.html)). OCCT shares the weakness: its implicit × parametric walker seeds closed loops from an interior grid, and its parametric × parametric walker seeds them from a polyhedral intersection ([IntWalk_IWalking.gxx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/master/src/ModelingAlgorithms/TKGeomAlgo/IntWalk/IntWalk_IWalking.gxx); [IntPatch_PrmPrmIntersection.cxx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/master/src/ModelingAlgorithms/TKGeomAlgo/IntPatch/IntPatch_PrmPrmIntersection.cxx)).

Every complete method finds *characteristic points* first. For the analytic × B-spline pairs that dominate cadrs' booleans, the implicit function of the plane, quadric or torus substituted into the patch gives a plane curve f(u,v) = 0. Its loop seeds are its **turning points {f = 0, f_v = 0} — every closed loop has at least two** — plus border roots and singular points {f = f_u = f_v = 0} ([node103](https://web.mit.edu/hyperbook/Patrikalakis-Maekawa-Cho/node103.html); [node105](https://web.mit.edu/hyperbook/Patrikalakis-Maekawa-Cho/node105.html); [Krishnan & Manocha 1996](papers/intersection/Krishnan-Manocha-1996-algebraic-loop-detection-GI.md)). That is a 2×2 Bernstein system on the uv box, the cheapest rigorous loop guarantee available, and it fits cadrs' `ssi` contour without changing its data flow. Split the box at the events, then contour monotone strips. For B-spline × B-spline, Hohmeyer's thesis proves that **if the two Gauss-map bounds are separable by a plane, no loop, singularity or interior isolated point can exist**. By Farkas' lemma this is the strongest criterion available from convex normal bounds: it passes two cylinders at any angle, where Sederberg's and Sinha's cone tests need arbitrarily fine subdivision. His plane-sweep trace (solve F = G with F·P = w, stepping w) has "exactly one solution" per plane and needs no careful step choice ([Hohmeyer 1992, read as page images](http://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/1992/Archive/CSD-92-681.pdf)). The Gauss-map bound of a uv box is cheap in closed form for cadrs' analytic surfaces. Tangential branches still need the second-fundamental-form quadratic for the marching direction, with its four discriminant cases ([Mukundan et al. 2004](papers/intersection/Mukundan-2004-tracing-surface-intersection-validated-ODE.md)), which OCCT also uses in `ComputeContactDirection` ([IntWalk_PWalking.cxx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/master/src/ModelingAlgorithms/TKGeomAlgo/IntWalk/IntWalk_PWalking.cxx)).

All of this needs one shared tool in `nurbs`: a Bernstein/B-spline box solver. It should prune sub-domains without a sign change, use Elber–Kim's normal-cone single-solution stop ([Elber & Kim 2001](papers/intersection/Elber-Kim-2001-geometric-constraint-solver-multivariate-splines.md)) and Mourrain–Pavone-style Jacobian preconditioning near double roots ([Fünfzig et al. 2010](papers/intersection/Fuenfzig-Michelucci-Foufou-2010-tensorial-Bernstein-solvers.md)), with the Liang–Mourrain–Pavone cell regularity test certifying contour topology ([Liang, Mourrain & Pavone 2008](papers/intersection/Liang-Mourrain-Pavone-2008-subdivision-topology-implicit-curves.md)). Such a solver serves curve/curve, curve/surface, point projection, `distance` stationary points, `silhouette` zero sets and every SSI characteristic point. Tensor Bernstein solvers become impractical beyond 6–7 variables, so this is a 2–4-variable tool. For enumeration that is a guarantee rather than a heuristic, the coefficients need rounded intervals. The 2025 TIGHT/NFG work gives correctly-rounded, SIMD-fast intervals and reports **~16x over Filib++ on torus–torus SSI** ([Sichetti, Attene & Puppo 2025](papers/kernels_recent/Sichetti-2025-TIGHT-intervals.md)). The fitting step has one clear win to copy from OCCT: `GeomInt_IntSS` fits the 3-D curve and **both p-curves simultaneously** in one least-squares pass (tolerance 1e-7, degree 4–8), so p-curves never need re-projection ([GeomInt_IntSS_1.cxx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/master/src/ModelingAlgorithms/TKGeomAlgo/GeomInt/GeomInt_IntSS_1.cxx)). Smaller exactness gains remain in the analytic pairs. Geometric conic detection for degenerate natural-quadric pairs is the robust alternative to factoring Levin's quartic, which "often fails … when it is singular" ([Trocado & Gonzalez-Vega 2019](papers/intersection/Computing-intersection-two-quadrics-2019-arxiv.md)). For tori, the Villarceau-circle cases are found by circle–circle and circle–line intersections only ([Kim & Kim 2000](papers/intersection/Kim-Kim-2000-torus-torus-conic-sections.md)), which matters when fillet tori meet other fillet tori.

## Booleans should re-intersect locally instead of retrying globally

cadrs' boolean is structurally the Jackson/OCCT pipeline, and on speed it already wins: **0.48 of OCCT on OCCT's cases and 0.17 on SMLib's** ([README](../crates/cadrs_kernel/README.md)). The literature's upgrades are therefore about robustness, and three of them converge. First, **skip the face–face intersection wherever lower-dimensional comparisons already found the contact**. Jackson's Parasolid paper says the surface intersector "need not consider curves … already known to be common by virtue of edge-edge or edge-face comparison. This can be important, since computation of near-tangent intersection curves can be very unstable" ([Jackson 1995](papers/booleans/Jackson-1995-local-tolerances.md)). OCCT's Glue modes do the same globally, with "up to 90%" speedups, and a fuzzy value of 6e-5 on edges deviating by 5.28e-5 gave a **stated 45x speedup** in one documented case ([OCCT boolean spec](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/overview/html/specification__boolean_operations.html)). ESOLID's profile, with curve–curve intersection at **54–98% of total time**, confirms that the intersection stage is where both cost and fragility live ([Keyser et al. 2002](papers/booleans/Keyser-2002-ESOLID-exact-boundary-evaluation.md)). Second, **re-intersect only what changed**. OCCT runs the new vertices and the drafted section edges through nested PaveFillers, and it gives each pave block a "shrunk range" (the range minus its end tolerance spheres) so that tiny split edges stop interfering ([OCCT spec](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/overview/html/specification__boolean_operations.html)). Jackson re-compares split edges "since the smaller edges may now be coincident". Segal records per entity a *near* value, the smallest distance at which an intersection was rejected, and backtracks only when a merge's new tolerance exceeds it ([Segal 1990](papers/booleans/Segal-1990-tolerances-polyhedral.md)). cadrs' retry of the whole boolean at 1e-5 and then 1e-4 after an open shell is a coarse version of all three, and the local form keeps tolerance growth confined to the entities around the gap. Segal's own warning applies: his tolerant modeller ran **5–10x slower even without backtracking**, and iterated unions collapsed a cube to a single vertex after 11 iterations. Growth has no published bound, so the 1e-4 ceiling should stay.

Third, coincident pieces need an explicit state. HHK's polyhedral boolean labels areas oninB (B's surface in the plane with the same orientation) and onoutB (opposite orientation), so unions and intersections keep one copy and subtractions drop both ([Hoffmann, Hopcroft & Karasick 1989](papers/booleans/Hoffmann-Hopcroft-Karasick-1989-robust-set-operations.md)). SolveSpace's COINC_SAME/COINC_OPP tags are the same rule in a curved kernel ([SolveSpace boolean.cpp](https://github.com/solvespace/solvespace/blob/master/src/srf/boolean.cpp)), and Zhou et al. argue for keeping duplicates tagged until extraction rather than merging during imprint ([Zhou et al. 2016](papers/booleans/Zhou-2016-mesh-arrangements.md)). Hoffmann's design rule ties the three together. Answer every incidence question through one or two *primary* predicates on the original geometry, and derive everything else from them, because "the same logical question may arise repeatedly, in the form of different floating-point computations giving contradictory answers" ([Hoffmann 2001](papers/booleans/Hoffmann-2001-robustness-in-geometric-computations.md)). Huang et al. apply the same principle after the fact. They snap face–face curve ends onto the edge–face hit of the shared boundary edge (the lower dimension has "lower errors"), and they collapse sub-tolerance intersection edges that are remnants of tangential touching. The rules come from an intersection graph built between intersection and imprint ([Huang et al. 2023](papers/booleans/Huang-2023-brep-boolean-repair.md)).

For `classify`, the mesh-boolean lineage gives a cascade cadrs can copy. Cherchi et al. cast **one axis-aligned ray per patch**, from a point verified to lie inside the patch, and perturb the far point with `nextafter` on vertex or edge hits. They fall back to exact arithmetic only when no valid start exists. In 80K rays, **97% succeeded at the first triangle and the exact fallback never ran** ([Cherchi et al. 2022](papers/booleans/Cherchi-2022-interactive-robust-mesh-booleans.md)). Zhou's per-cell winding-number *vector* lets one arrangement serve union, intersection, difference, "at least k of n" and `cells` without re-classifying ([Zhou et al. 2016](papers/booleans/Zhou-2016-mesh-arrangements.md)), the same idea as OCCT's Cells Builder. For inputs whose shells still do not close, generalized winding numbers on exact trimmed NURBS classify robustly through gaps at **0.15–1.29 ms per query** ([Spainhour & Weiss 2025](papers/kernels_recent/Spainhour-2025-Containment-trimmed-NURBS-winding-numbers.md)). That makes them a fallback for `classify` and `meshsolid`, not the main path. Exact predicates pay off only on the planar subset: orient2d/orient3d for uv loops, planar faces and plane–plane–plane vertices ([Shewchuk 1997](papers/booleans/Shewchuk-1997-adaptive-precision-DCG.md)). For curved geometry, exactness is out: separating quadric intersections needs an estimated **L^720 bits** of precision ([Hoffmann 2001](papers/booleans/Hoffmann-2001-robustness-in-geometric-computations.md)).

On the tolerance model itself (`tol`), Jackson supplies the validity test `Body::check` lacks for tolerant bodies. Tolerance regions of non-adjacent entities must not intersect, and each connected component of the overlap of two adjacent entities' regions must be contractible onto a common lower-dimensional entity, with vertex ≥ edge ≥ face tolerance. Jackson also tests coincidence with the **sum** of the two entities' tolerances ([Jackson 1995](papers/booleans/Jackson-1995-local-tolerances.md)), while the README says cadrs' operations "take the larger of their own and the entities'". Using the larger value can miss a coincidence Parasolid would detect between two tolerant bodies.

## Offsets, sweeps, lofts and drafts: restrict first, generalise second

Offsets are already cadrs' strength (**0.17 of OCCT**), so the offset literature mainly offers robustness and boolean-count reduction for `offset_round` and `slab`. Rossignac & Requicha's pruning rules say that the offset boundary needs tubes only on edges that are singular in the right convexity, and "BRep edges that separate tangent faces are not singular curves … and therefore never need to be n-offset" ([Rossignac & Requicha 1986](papers/offsets_sweeps_lofts/Rossignac-Requicha-1986-offsetting-operations-in-solid-modelling.md)). cadrs already restricts tubes to convex edges outward and concave edges inward. The remaining saving is dropping tubes on smooth edges and balls at vertices whose edges are all smooth or of the wrong convexity, which on filleted parts the offsets researcher expects to be "probably a large share" of the booleans (an unmeasured estimate). For the sharp path in `local`, Parasolid's documentation gives the repair vocabulary. When the distance exceeds the minimum radius of curvature, a wholly inside-out face is deleted ("blends whose radius is less than the offset distance are removed"). Swept and spun faces are cut at one new edge per self-intersection. Neighbours are extended to close gaps, and vertices with more than three edges are split ([Parasolid v12 ch. 19](http://www.q-solid.com/Parasolid_Docs/chapters/fd_chap.20.html); [V35 ch. 58](http://www.q-solid.com/Parasolid_Docs_V35/chapters/fd_chap.059.html)). The trigger is a per-face principal-radius pre-test, since the offset's curvature is 1/(ρ + d) ([Patrikalakis & Maekawa lecture 13](papers/offsets_sweeps_lofts/Patrikalakis-Maekawa-2003-MIT-lecture-13-offset-curves-surfaces.md)). The same list is the checklist for what `Move face` currently refuses (commit 57657d8).

For **drafts** (1.9x), the obvious split is to try OCCT's restricted case first in `local`. A planar face rotated about its trace on the neutral plane stays a plane, a cylinder becomes a cone, and neighbours are re-intersected as conics with no topology change ([OCCT DraftAngle](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/refman/html/class_b_rep_offset_a_p_i___draft_angle.html)). Only when that fails does the SMLib-style general path run. The general case follows Parasolid's two stages: imprint isocline curves to split faces into steep and non-steep regions, then replace the drafted surfaces with isocline surfaces about a reference edge, face or surface, choosing among multiple solutions by "help geometry" ([Parasolid v12 ch. 20](http://www.q-solid.com/Parasolid_Docs/chapters/fd_chap.21.html)). No academic paper on draft construction was found.

For **sweeps** (2.4x), the frame method is already state of the art: cadrs uses the double-reflection RMF, which has **O(h⁴) global error against the second-order projection and rotation methods at comparable per-frame cost** ([Wang, Jüttler, Zheng & Liu 2007 TR](papers/offsets_sweeps_lofts/Wang-Juttler-Zheng-Liu-2007-computation-of-rotation-minimizing-frames-TR.md)). The lever in `sweep::frames` is choosing the sample count from that bound and the spine's curvature rather than a fixed step, plus the paper's boundary-condition extension for closed spines and corner joins. For `sweep::corner`, OCCT's RightCorner and RoundCorner preconditions are the checks to run before choosing a join. RightCorner is valid only if the extended pieces meet in a connected planar curve; RoundCorner is guaranteed only for profiles orthogonal to the spine ([OCCT MakePipeShell header](https://eic-code-browser.sdcc.bnl.gov/lxr/source/include/opencascade/BRepOffsetAPI_MakePipeShell.hxx)). A local fold pre-check, κ(s)·(p·N(s)) ≥ 1 for a profile point at or beyond the centre of curvature, is the formal version of the practitioner rule ([Shapr3D forum](https://discourse.shapr3d.com/t/loft-sweep-error-swept-body-has-self-intersection/13041)). The researcher derived it, and no source quotes it. For **lofts**, the missing feature in `loft_build` is guide curves. Gordon surfaces in B-spline form are skin(profiles) + skin(guides) − tensor interpolant of the intersection grid, which requires a compatible network and the same parameters, degrees and knots in all three interpolations ([Siggel et al. 2019](papers/offsets_sweeps_lofts/Siggel-et-al-2019-TiGL-Gordon-surface-curve-network-interpolation.md)); OCCT 8.0 added `GeomFill_Gordon` ([8.0 release](https://github.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/releases/tag/V8_0_0)). For incompatible sections, Piegl–Tiller approximate skinning on a common knot vector with knot removal cut one example from **81×1455 to 81×48 control points** ([Piegl research page](https://cse.usf.edu/~lespiegl/research.htm)). The originals are paywalled.

## Mass properties close their 11x gap with exact per-span rules; the queries need better bounds

`mass` has the clearest upgrade path in the whole survey. For a non-rational B-spline face of bidegree (p, q), the volume integrand x·(S_u × S_v) is a polynomial of bidegree (3p−1, 3q−1) per knot span. Gauss–Legendre with ⌈3p/2⌉ × ⌈3q/2⌉ points per span is then **exact**: a fixed rule with zero error, which is OCCT's cost model with a correctness guarantee. This degree argument is the tessellation researcher's derivation from the isogeometric quadrature literature ([Hughes, Reali & Sangalli 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2008.12.004)), not a quoted result, though it is elementary to check. Cells cut by trims take the Green's-theorem path. Gunderman, Weiss & Evans show that treating the inner integral as numerical antidifferentiation with its own Gauss order, and integrating trim pieces in their own parameter, gives **exponential convergence to machine precision for exactly represented trims** ([Gunderman, Weiss & Evans 2021](papers/tessellation_mass_queries/Gunderman-Weiss-Evans-2021-mesh-free-quadrature-trimmed-parametric-surfaces.md)). Planar faces with straight edges and `meshsolid` bodies should use the closed polyhedral forms, which for triangles need no projection or Green's theorem at all ([Eberly 2002](papers/tessellation_mass_queries/Eberly-2002-polyhedral-mass-properties.md)). Exact Bernstein-product moments (Gonzalez-Ochoa, McCammon & Peters 1998, [DOI 10.1145/285857.285858](https://doi.org/10.1145/285857.285858)) make the right test oracle, though the description in the notes is from memory. Reporting error as the difference between two rule orders keeps results comparable with OCCT's adaptive mode. SMLib's own known-issues list, **1.4% mass error on faces whose trims are rational circles with interior knots**, is a free regression case ([usd-brep KNOWN_ISSUES](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/usd-brep/main/KNOWN_ISSUES.md)).

`tess` is at parity (0.97). OCCT's remaining advantage is its per-surface-type range splitters, analytic iso-lines for cylinder, cone, sphere and torus, which give regular interior nodes without a deflection-control iteration ([OCCT mesh guide](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/overview/html/occt_user_guides__mesh.html)). The principled upgrade is Delaunay refinement driven by a sizing field: insert circumcentres only in triangles failing the chord bound or the angle bound ([Shewchuk 2002](papers/tessellation_mass_queries/Shewchuk-2002-Delaunay-refinement-triangular-mesh-generation.md)). The field comes from a pointwise anisotropic metric, h_i = √(8ε/κ_i), or from a guaranteed control-net bound ([Balázs, Guthe & Klein 2004](papers/tessellation_mass_queries/Balazs-Guthe-Klein-2004-efficient-trimmed-NURBS-tessellation.md)). GPU CDT pays off only for millions of points, so it is not worth pursuing for CAD faces.

For `distance`, Johnson & Cohen's LUB-tree is the direct refinement of cadrs' branch and bound on cell boxes. It searches breadth-first with early upper bounds from surface points, takes lower bounds from convex hulls of B-spline pieces, refines only active regions by knot insertion, and reuses bounds between queries ("more than double the speed") ([Johnson & Cohen 1998](papers/tessellation_mass_queries/Johnson-Cohen-1998-framework-efficient-minimum-distance.md); [1999](papers/tessellation_mass_queries/Johnson-Cohen-1999-bound-coherence-minimum-distance.md)). The tangent-cone pruning of their 2005 paper is from memory. `max_distance` needs event enumeration: antipodal and boundary systems solved by a subdivision solver, the only published exact method ([Elber & Grandine 2008](papers/tessellation_mass_queries/Elber-Grandine-2008-Hausdorff-minimal-distances-freeforms.md)). Hu–Wallner's curvature-based projection step is a cheap swap for damped Newton with a better basin, but its description is from memory because the author's server was down. For `hlr` and `silhouette`, three published improvements apply. Split silhouettes at cusps computed from curvature ([Hertzmann & Zorin 2000](papers/tessellation_mass_queries/Hertzmann-Zorin-2000-illustrating-smooth-surfaces.md)). Replace per-piece ray tests with one ray per chain plus Appel's quantitative-invisibility propagation. Index projected curves in 2D instead of comparing all pairs, which is the O(E·F) weakness of OCCT's "every edge against every face" `HLRBRep_Algo` ([OCCT HLR](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/refman/html/class_h_l_r_b_rep___algo.html); [Bénard & Hertzmann 2019](papers/tessellation_mass_queries/Benard-Hertzmann-2019-line-drawings-from-3D-models-tutorial.md)). The README gives no HLR performance numbers, so these are design rather than gap items.

## STEP import, healing and naming: copy OCCT's shortcuts, then fix the split-face weak spot

For `step`/`xbuild`, profiling has to separate lexing from model building before anything else changes. Then copy OCCT's shortcuts: recompute p-curves only on planes, keep SameParameter off by default, and run fixes "only if needed" ([OCCT STEP guide](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/overview/html/occt_user_guides__step.html)). After that, build faces in parallel with rayon before the single-threaded sewing step. The only published OCCT-beating result is a hierarchical thread-pool parser in the OpenGE engine, **about 2x its serial version and ~1.5x faster than OpenCASCADE**. That figure is from the abstract only; the full text is in Chinese and unread ([Bi et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3724/SP.J.1089.2023.2023-00037)). OCCT's sewing is the only fully specified sewing algorithm found, and it gives `xbuild` concrete checks. It cuts free bounds at other edges' projected vertices before pairing, finds candidates through a UB-tree of vertex boxes, samples 8 points per edge pair, keeps the *longer* edge as reference, and sets the vertex tolerance to the smallest sphere enclosing both vertex spheres ([BRepBuilderAPI_Sewing.cxx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/V7_8_0/src/BRepBuilderAPI/BRepBuilderAPI_Sewing.cxx)). Every OCCT tolerance increase is capped by a `MaxTolerance` ("a problem needing a larger value is not fixed"), except in SameParameter ([shape healing guide](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/overview/html/occt_user_guides__shape_healing.html)). cadrs' recognition of analytic surfaces lacks the torus case, which OCCT builds from circle/circle iso-curves verified on a 20×20 grid at `min(tol, diag/1000)` ([GeomConvert_SurfToAnaSurf.cxx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/V7_8_0/src/GeomConvert/GeomConvert_SurfToAnaSurf.cxx)). It also lacks a least-squares fallback; Lukács, Martin & Marshall's faithful distance functions let a torus fit degrade gracefully into a sphere, cone or cylinder ([Lukács, Martin & Marshall 1998](papers/exchange_healing_naming/Lukacs-1998-faithful-fitting.md)). Recognising imported rolling-ball blends would let `fillet` re-blend STEP models. Siemens' expired-in-2025 patent on blend recognition and chain sorting describes it ([US8326583](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8326583B2/en)).

Persistent naming lives in `cadrs_core`'s `naming.rs`, but it depends on what the kernel records in its history and on how `unify` merges faces. The surveys agree that ordering-based split names (Wu's PSI, Mun & Han's OSI, FreeCAD's OCCT-report order, cadrs' centroid order) are the weakest family. FreeCAD's own wiki documents a fillet that "bound to the lower of two split edges, because OCCT reported it first" ([RealThunder algorithm page](https://github.com/realthunder/FreeCAD_assembly3/wiki/Topological-Naming-Algorithm)). The defensible alternatives match split pieces by **how much of the old boundary circuit they inherit** ([Agbodan et al. 2003](papers/exchange_healing_naming/Agbodan-2003-topological-entity-matching.md)), or by dependency records with wildcard id components and external disambiguation, as Onshape describes ([Onshape forum](https://forum.onshape.com/discussion/comment/130171)). For merges, keeping the list of merged names (Mun & Han's secondary name) instead of cadrs' "the smaller name wins" lets a reference to either input survive and un-merge cleanly ([Mun & Han 2005](papers/exchange_healing_naming/Mun-Han-2005-naming-mapping-parametric-exchange.md)). Bidarra et al. go further and forbid merging coplanar faces of different owners at all ([Bidarra et al. 2005](papers/exchange_healing_naming/Bidarra-2005-feature-based-persistent-naming.md)). That conflicts directly with `unify`, so it is a policy decision rather than a drop-in.

## Other kernels compete on configuration catalogues and diagnostics, not new cores

Vendor material from 2015–2026 publishes blend *configurations*, threading modes and diagnostics, not algorithms. Parasolid's release notes list apex-range chamfers, overflow and capping diagnostics, notch blends and partially successful G1 hole patches that return a failure list ([Parasolid v31.1](https://blogs.sw.siemens.com/plm-components/parasolid-version-31-1-release-highlights/); [v32.0](https://blogs.sw.siemens.com/plm-components/parasolid-v32-0-release-highlights/)). C3D documents rolling-ball and rolling-disk fillets, a "create, then trim and stitch" face-fillet flow, patching of self-intersecting fillets, and operation logging that dumps inputs before execution, the same idea as `CADRS_DUMP_DIR` ([C3D 2025](https://c3dlabs.com/blog/products/c3d-geometric-kernel-new-features-and-development-trends/)). Zoo's GPU SSI densely samples both surfaces, then thins the uv band with convolutions. It admits SSI "depends critically on the sampling" and had published no benchmarks as of October 2026 ([Zoo engine overview](https://zoo.dev/research/zoo-cad-engine-overview)), which makes it a cautionary tale rather than a model. The open Rust kernels give no precedent: truck seeds intersections from meshes and supports only transversal booleans, and Fornjot has shut down ([truck shapeops lib.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ricosjp/truck/master/truck-shapeops/src/lib.rs); [Fornjot](https://github.com/hannobraun/fornjot)). The most useful recent event is the **Apache-2.0 release of SMLib as NVIDIA's usd-brep** ([GitHub](https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/usd-brep)). cadrs' `fillet` and `local` already follow SMLib's fillet executive and local operation, and its known-issues list doubles as a healing and mass-property test list. The academic frontier relevant to cadrs is *certification*: interval and Bernstein bounds for SSI, silhouettes, tessellation validity and distance. Nothing comparable exists in OCCT or, publicly, in the commercial kernels. For a kernel gate, ABC (about 1M Onshape STEP models filtered to need no healing) and the Fusion 360 segmentation set (42,912 STEP files with faces labelled by creating feature, fillets and chamfers included) are directly usable inputs ([Koch et al. 2019](papers/kernels_recent/Koch-2019-ABC-big-CAD-model-dataset.md); [Fusion 360 Gallery](https://github.com/AutodeskAILab/Fusion360GalleryDataset)). Neither is a robustness benchmark with expected results, because no such public benchmark exists.

## Where the researchers disagree, and which claims rest on thin evidence

The blends researcher thinks cadrs' fillet gap lies in contact-curve tracing and fitting, imprinting and the tool-body fallback, "more likely … than in a missing closed form". The intersection researcher attributes the fillet and sweep slowness to B-spline × analytic and B-spline × B-spline SSI. Neither profiled the kernel, which is why profiling ranks first. The booleans notes say the "sum of tolerances" test is "already the cadrs model". The exchange notes and the README ("the larger of their own and the entities'") say cadrs uses the maximum. The README is the primary source, so the report treats sum-versus-max as an open decision. The two booleans-adjacent notes describe OCCT's vertex/vertex merge differently: the booleans researcher writes "the centre of the sphere enclosing the source tolerance spheres", and the kernels researcher writes "the midpoint with tol = max(tol_i, tol_j) + D/2". Both cite the same spec, and the sewing source's `ComputeToleranceVertex` is the exact enclosing sphere. On classification, Krishnan's BOOLE needs only **two rays per boolean** thanks to adjacency propagation ([Krishnan 1997](papers/booleans/Krishnan-1997-dissertation-boundary-evaluation-sculptured-solids.md)), while Cherchi reports per-patch rays **up to 100x faster than** Zhou-style patch-graph propagation on meshes. Which wins for a curved B-rep with few, large faces is untested. The offsets researcher recommends making re-intersection the default offset strategy, but cadrs' boolean-based offsets are already 6x faster than OCCT, so that argument is about robustness only. The notes also disagree on citation details. Jackson 1995 carries two DOIs (10.1145/218013.218069 in the booleans notes, 10.1145/218013.218072 in the kernels notes; the exchange notes could not resolve one). The booleans notes attribute arXiv:2302.14373, "A note on solid modeling", to C. M. Hoffmann, but the downloaded file is byte-identical to the offsets notes' copy, which is by **Zou Qiang (Zhejiang University), in Chinese**. Zou is the correct author.

Several algorithm descriptions rest on recollection or secondary sources and should be read in the original before anyone builds on them. These include Grandine & Klein's turning-point system and BVP tracing; Bajaj et al.'s 1988 tracing (the PDF is an image scan); Forsyth 1995's shelling algorithm, which the offsets researcher explicitly says not to state as fact; and the Filip–Magedson–Markot chord bound formula. The list continues with Gonzalez-Ochoa's exact moments, Hu–Wallner's projection step, Piegl & Richard's tessellation, Johnson & Cohen 2005's tangent-cone pruning, Bartoň–Elber's no-loop tests, Sederberg–Nishita clipping details, and the Gauss orders OCCT's non-adaptive mass path uses. Hohmeyer's theorems were read from page images of a scanned thesis. The exact per-span Gauss orders, the sweep fold condition and the Rossignac pruning share are researcher derivations or estimates. Single-source, self-reported numbers include openbim-step's tokenizer throughput, Bi et al.'s 1.5x over OCCT (abstract only), OCCT's 45x fuzzy speed-up (one documented case), and Liang/Huang 2025's "comparable to Parasolid and ACIS" boolean (Chinese body text not extracted, [JCAD 2025](https://www.jcad.cn/en/article/pdf/preview/10.3724/SP.J.1089.2024-00367.pdf)).

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which better algorithm is cadrs missing" to "which documented restriction or shortcut is cadrs not taking, and which guarantee can it add that OCCT does not have". For drafts, mass properties, STEP import and probably sweeps, OCCT's advantage is an explicit narrowing of the problem, and cadrs can take the same narrow fast path while keeping its exact general path behind it. Mass properties are the only place where cadrs can match OCCT's speed *and* stay exact. Fillets remain the open item, and the field offers no benchmark to say where the time goes.

The longer-term opportunity is certification. A pure-Rust kernel that pairs one Bernstein box solver with correctly-rounded intervals can prove that an SSI contour missed no loop, that a silhouette has the right topology and that a distance is the global minimum. No public kernel claims those properties. Combined with Jackson's local, sum-of-tolerances re-intersection in place of global retries, this would give cadrs a robustness story that goes past matching OCCT. The cost is concentrated in a few shared pieces (the solver, the interval type, the local re-intersection loop), which is why they rank above the many feature-level items.

## About this folder
- `FINDINGS.md` is the short version: the most valuable leads, the corrections, and the state of the paper collection. `download.md` lists the papers still missing.
- `README.md` is this report. `notes/` holds the per-area research notes it was written from, one file per area, each with a ranked reading list.
- `algorithms/` holds the implementation cards distilled from the research, with a cross-area build order in `algorithms/README.md`. Where a card corrects this report, the card is right: inertia Gauss counts, sum versus max tolerances, and OCCT's fillet constants.
- `papers/<area>/` holds the open-access papers the notes cite. The PDFs are git-ignored; each has a Markdown conversion beside it with the same name, made with docling (`~/tools/docling`), and a plain-text extraction (`.txt`, `pdftotext -layout`). The `.md` has clean reading order, headings and tables, but its display equations are `<!-- formula-not-decoded -->` placeholders. The `.txt` keeps the equation symbols, laid out as on the page, with two-column text side by side. Use the `.md` to read and the `.txt` for the maths.
- Scanned PDFs were converted with OCR (RapidOCR), so expect letter errors in those, such as Bajaj et al. 1988, Segal and Séquin 1988, Hohmeyer 1992, and the patents.
- To convert a newly added PDF:

```sh
~/tools/docling/bin/docling convert papers/<area>/<file>.pdf --to md --ocr \
  --image-export-mode placeholder --device cuda --output papers/<area>/
```
