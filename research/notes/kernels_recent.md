# Recent B-rep CAD kernels (2015–2026): open-source kernels, commercial vendors, kernel-level academic work, benchmarks

Scope: what other B-rep kernels do and which new algorithmic ideas have appeared since 2015, for `cadrs_kernel` (`crates/cadrs_kernel/README.md`, "The native kernel"). Sibling notes cover booleans, blends, intersection, offsets/sweeps/lofts, tessellation/mass/queries and exchange/healing in depth; this file is the kernel-level survey and points to them where a topic is theirs.

Method: web search (the session's shared search budget ran out about two thirds of the way through, after which pages were fetched directly from known URLs and from the reference lists of downloaded papers), primary docs and source, arXiv/author/institutional PDFs. Every claim has an inline source. Items that could not be verified are marked.

PDFs (all verified to begin with `%PDF`) are in `../papers/kernels_recent/`; each entry below lists its local path.

## Top 5 "must read" (ranked)

1. **OCCT Boolean Operations specification (General Fuse)** — the only complete, public description of a production boolean (data structure, interference order, tolerance rules, options, limitations), and the kernel cadrs benchmarks against. Entry 1.1 below. Informs `boolean`, `split`, `classify`, `unify`, `tol`.
2. **Jackson 1995, "Boundary representation modelling with local tolerances" (Parasolid)** — still the only primary account of tolerant modelling: tolerant edges as p-curve bundles, contractibility validity rules, raise-locally-and-merge instead of global retry. Entry 2.1. Informs `tol`, `topo`, `boolean`.
3. **Spainhour & Weiss 2025/2026, generalized winding numbers over trimmed NURBS (TOG)** — exact-surface inside/outside test robust to gaps, embarrassingly parallel; a replacement or fallback for ray-parity classification. Entry 3.B1. Informs `classify`, `boolean_kinds`, GPU spike.
4. **Sichetti, Attene & Puppo 2025, TIGHT intervals (+ NFG, MiSo)** — correctly-rounded, SIMD-fast interval arithmetic demonstrated on torus–torus SSI; the practical route to validated `ssi`/`distance`. Entry 3.C1.
5. **Cherchi et al. 2022, Interactive and Robust Mesh Booleans (+ Attene 2020 indirect predicates)** — unevaluated intersection points plus filtered exact predicates, cells labelled once by winding number; the mesh-boolean idea that transfers to a curved-B-rep split/classify pipeline. Entry 3.F1/3.D1. Informs `split`, `classify`, `cells`, `tess`.

Runners-up: Zoo CAD Engine Overview + Rockwood patent (the only public GPU-first SSI in a shipping kernel, 2.2); C3D Labs' 2025–2026 kernel posts (the most algorithmically specific vendor writing, 2.3); SolveSpace's `srf/` source (a small exact-plus-marched NURBS boolean, 1.4); OCCT 8.0.x release notes (what a 25-year-old kernel changed for speed, 1.1).

## Key question 1: Open-source B-rep kernels and their algorithm documentation

### Takeaway
Only OCCT documents a full B-rep pipeline (booleans, mesh, healing) in public prose; its 8.0 release (2026) is mainly a performance/architecture rewrite, not new algorithms. The Rust kernels are either shut down (Fornjot) or mesh-seeded and transversal-only (truck). SolveSpace, BRL-CAD and SMLib/usd-brep are the three other open code bases with real curved-surface booleans; SMLib (Apache-2.0 since 2026) is the closest in design to cadrs (cadrs' fillet already follows "SMLib's fillet executive") but its documentation is API-level only. CGAL and libfive are exact-polyhedral and implicit respectively; nothing in them does curved B-rep booleans.

### Cited Findings (entries)

#### 1.1 OpenCASCADE Technology (OCCT) — Open Cascade SAS
- URLs: docs https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/overview/html/ (dev.opencascade.org redirects there); Boolean spec https://github.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/blob/master/dox/specification/boolean_operations/boolean_operations.md ; mesh guide https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/overview/html/occt_user_guides__mesh.html ; shape healing https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/overview/html/occt_user_guides__shape_healing.html ; modeling algorithms https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/overview/html/occt_user_guides__modeling_algos.html ; releases https://github.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/releases
- Licence: LGPL 2.1 only with exception (since 6.7.0, Dec 2013; Fedora regarded the earlier OCCT Public License as non-free) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Cascade_Technology).
- No open-access paper; the docs above are the primary material. (The 7.3.0 release-notes PDF at old.opencascade.com returned HTML, not downloaded.)

**Booleans (General Fuse, `BOPAlgo_PaveFiller` + `BOPAlgo_Builder`)** — [spec](https://github.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/blob/master/dox/specification/boolean_operations/boolean_operations.md): All operators (Boolean, Splitter, Section, Cells Builder, Volume Maker) share one Data Structure and Intersection Part and differ only in the Building Part. The DS holds paves (vertex index + parameter on a curve), pave blocks (split-edge parametric ranges, with a *shrunk range* = range minus the vertex tolerance spheres; only the shrunk range can interfere), common blocks (coincident pave blocks and the faces they lie on), per-face FaceInfo (In/On pave blocks and vertices) and FF interferences carrying section curves with their own tolerances. Two shapes interfere when the distance between their geometries is at most the sum of their tolerances; six B-rep pair types (VV, VE, VF, EE, EF, FF) plus solid types (VZ, EZ, FZ, ZZ), computed lower dimension first in the order VV, VE, EE, VF, EF, FF, VZ, EZ, FZ, ZZ. VV makes one vertex at the midpoint with tol = max(tol_i, tol_j) + D/2; VE/VF grow the vertex tolerance to the projection distance plus the edge/face tolerance; EE/EF yield common blocks or new vertices; FF yields section curves or isolated-point vertices. New vertices from EE are re-merged in a *nested* PaveFiller; section edges are drafted from FF curves and intersected in another nested PaveFiller; p-curves are then built on both faces. Same-domain (coincident) vertices, edges and faces are chained and one representative kept. Building goes vertices → edges → wires → faces (`BOPAlgo_BuilderFace`) → shells → solids (`FillIn3DParts` classification, `BOPAlgo_BuilderSolid`), then `CorrectPointOnCurve` / `CorrectCurveOnSurface` fix tolerances so the result passes `BRepCheck_Analyzer`. Fuse keeps Out parts and removes internal faces, Common keeps In/On, Cut12 keeps Out of S1 w.r.t. S2. Options: **Fuzzy** (an extra tolerance applied on top of entity tolerances during intersection; one documented case shows a ~45× speed-up), **Gluing** (GlueShift skips FF; GlueFull skips VF, EF and FF; coincidence is not verified), **Non-destructive/safe** (inputs untouched, so one prepared PaveFiller can serve several operations), **CheckInverted** (skip negative-volume classification, up to ~10%), **OBB** tighter boxes, **RunParallel**, history (Generated only for pure intersections: VERTEX from E/F, EDGE from F/F). Cells Builder computes cells once and selects by take/avoid lists and material; `RemoveInternalBoundaries` merges same-material cells. `SimplifyResult` is `ShapeUpgrade_UnifySameDomain` with the fuzzy value as linear tolerance. Alerts live in `BOPAlgo_Alerts.hxx` (e.g. `AlertNullInputShapes`, `AlertTooSmallEdge`).
- Stated limitations: arguments must be valid and *not self-interfered* (BRepCheck is necessary, not sufficient); faces/edges at least C1; Fuse needs equal dimensions; parametric precision loss on cylinders/cones at large scales; vertex tolerances that bridge gaps leave the edge trajectory undefined; edges on different curve types give vertices not common blocks; overlapping tolerance zones can force impossible topology; "acquired self-interference"; operations on open solids cannot always be guaranteed. — [spec](https://github.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/blob/master/dox/specification/boolean_operations/boolean_operations.md)

**BRepMesh (rewritten in 7.4)** — [mesh guide](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/overview/html/occt_user_guides__mesh.html): stages are model builder → edge discretisation (3D + 2D, reused if within parameters) → model healer (wire closure and self-intersection in parametric space, resolved by refining deflection up to five iterations or by splitting edges; unresolved faces are flagged self-intersecting and skipped) → preprocessor (drop inconsistent existing triangulations) → face discretisation in 2D by a per-surface-type factory (Delaunay `BRepMesh_DelaunayBaseMeshAlgo` default, a sweep-line variant, and a `BRepMesh_DelabellaMeshAlgoFactory` alternative) → internal node insertion by range splitters (none for planes, analytic splitters for cone/cylinder/sphere, UV splitters from boundary discretisation for torus/NURBS) → `BRepMesh_DelaunayDeflectionControlMeshAlgo` checks triangles against deflection → post-processing writes polygons to edges. Parameters: Deflection (clamped up to the shape tolerance), Angle (12–20° suggested), DeflectionInterior, AngleInterior (B-spline faces only), Relative, InParallel (edges and faces), MinSize, InternalVerticesMode, ControlSurfaceDeflection. The stage tools (`IMeshTools_Context`, `IMeshData` model, `BRepMesh_ModelBuilder/EdgeDiscret/ModelHealer/FaceDiscret/ModelPostProcessor`) are replaceable; third-party 2D meshers plug in via `BRepMesh_ConstrainedBaseMeshAlgo` / `CustomBaseMeshAlgo`.

**Shape healing** — [guide](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/overview/html/occt_user_guides__shape_healing.html): `ShapeFix_Wire::Perform` order is reorder → small edges → connected → edge curves (pcurves, 3D curves, seams, SameParameter) → degenerated → self-intersection (move the shared vertex, or raise the nearest vertex tolerance) → lacking edges. Three repair modes (raise a tolerance, change topology, change geometry) chosen by flags; `MaxTolerance` caps increases except in SameParameter / FixVertexTolerance; working precision default 1e-7. `ShapeFix_Face` orients wires and adds missing seams; `ShapeFix_Shell` makes orientation coherent. `ShapeUpgrade` splits by continuity, closed faces, area, angle, and C0 B-splines at degree-multiplicity knots; `UnifySameDomain` merges tangent faces/edges.

**Fillets (ChFi3d)** — headers only, no prose: [ChFi3d_Builder.hxx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/V7_9_0/src/ChFi3d/ChFi3d_Builder.hxx), [ChFi3d_FilBuilder.hxx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/V7_9_0/src/ChFi3d/ChFi3d_FilBuilder.hxx). Edges are grouped into contours (`ChFiDS_Spine`), each with a `ChFiDS_Stripe` of `ChFiDS_SurfData`; `PerformSurf` overloads for face/face, edge/face and edge/edge contact; `ComputeData` marches a contact line with `Blend_Function` / `Blend_FuncInv` / `BRepBlend_Line`; `StoreData` approximates it via `AppBlend_Approx` (so OCCT fillet faces are B-spline approximations, not exact); corners are `PerformOneCorner` / `TwoCorner` / `ThreeCorner` / `MoreThreeCorner` / `SingularCorner` / `FilletOnVertex` / `IntersectMoreCorner` / `IntersectionAtEnd`; `ExtentOneCorner/Two/Three` extend stripes past vertices; the result is sewn through `TopOpeBRepDS_HDataStructure` + `TopOpeBRepBuild_HBuilder`; `StripeStatus` is WalkingFailure / TwistedSurface / Error / Ok. `ChFi3d_FilBuilder` takes a fillet shape (Rational default, QuasiAngular, Polynomial) and default tolerance 1e-2; radius constant, per edge/vertex/parameter, or a `Law`. User guide: an edge must be shared by two faces, fillets propagate along tangent-continuous edges, variable radius by (parameter, radius) arrays; chamfers two-distance; 7.4 added "constant throat" chamfers — [modeling algos](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/overview/html/occt_user_guides__modeling_algos.html), [7.4 release](https://github.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/releases/tag/V7_4_0).

**Offsets** — [modeling algos](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/overview/html/occt_user_guides__modeling_algos.html): `MakeOffsetShape::PerformByJoin` offsets each face's surface, computes analytical continuations, intersects pairs according to the original topology and assembles the shell (join Arc or Intersection; Intersection/SelfInter flags; RemoveIntEdges); `PerformBySimple` maps surfaces to `Geom_OffsetSurface`, keeps p-curves, re-approximates 3D curves from the p-curve on the first face, averages vertices and grows tolerances (needs smooth input). `MakeThickSolidByJoin` removes faces and rounds or intersects by edge convexity. 8.0 lets `Geom_OffsetSurface` carry an equivalent non-offset surface to bypass offset evaluation — [8.0 release](https://github.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/releases/tag/V8_0_0). 7.7 added a 2D offset option that approximates contours by arcs and lines "for more stable work" — [7.7 release](https://github.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/releases/tag/V7_7_0).

**HLR, defeaturing** — `HLRBRep_Algo` (exact: every edge vs every face; outlines and isoparameters) and `HLRBRep_PolyAlgo` (polyhedral); points and infinite faces are not processed. `BOPAlgo_RemoveFeatures` (7.3+): group feature faces into connected blocks, extend adjacent faces, trim them to original bounds, rebuild; fails if adjacent faces are tangent or the extensions do not cover the feature. — [modeling algos](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/overview/html/occt_user_guides__modeling_algos.html)

**Release history, algorithm items 2015–2026** — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Cascade_Technology), [7.4](https://github.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/releases/tag/V7_4_0), [7.6](https://github.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/releases/tag/V7_6_0), [7.7](https://github.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/releases/tag/V7_7_0), [7.8](https://github.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/releases/tag/V7_8_0), [7.9](https://occt3d.com/dev/content/open-cascade-technology-790-released/index.html), [8.0](https://github.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/releases/tag/V8_0_0):
  - 6.9 (2015): Fuzzy booleans; SSI and curve-on-surface projection robustness; built-in parallel tools.
  - 7.2 (2017): Glue option. 7.3 (2018): boolean, SSI and offset optimisation; OBB.
  - 7.4 (2019): BRepMesh rewrite (robustness, performance, accuracy; interior deflection controls); booleans on open solids; history suppression; result simplification; legacy booleans removed; constant-throat chamfers; `OSD_Parallel` and BVH traversal tools; properties of triangulation-only shapes.
  - 7.6 (2021): progress/abort for booleans, offset, GeomPlate, DistShapeShape; multithreaded `BRepCheck_Analyzer`; triangulation stores its deflection; scaled transforms banned from locations.
  - 7.7 (2022): canonical geometry recognition (plane/cylinder/cone/sphere, line/circle/ellipse within tolerance); arc+line 2D offset; many BRepMesh fixes.
  - 7.8 (2023): pluggable memory manager (jemalloc "up to 40%" on large files); STEP thread-safety; canonical geoplane detection.
  - 7.9 (2025): boolean robustness; sphere cutting; offset degenerate curves; `IntPatch` closed curves; `UnifySameDomain` fixes for revolution/extrusion; `DE_ShapeFixParameters`.
  - 8.0 (May 2026; 8.0.1 July 2026): C++17; **`EvalD0..EvalDN` are the primary virtuals with POD results, all 29 `Geom` leaf classes `final`, elementary evaluation devirtualised via `std::variant`**; B-spline arrays stored by value with a static unit-weight buffer; `BRepGraph` topology; `GeomProp`/`BRepProp`; `GeomFill_Gordon` transfinite interpolation (parallel, KKT saddle-point interpolation constraints, C2 periodic closure); `IntPatch_InterferencePolyhedron` on a BVH (O(log n)); `IntAna_QuadQuadGeo` near-parallel test; cylinder–cylinder tangent-along-a-line rerouted to implicit–implicit; `IntWalk_PWalking` closes lines returning near their start; new `ExtremaPC` grid bracketing; `BRepGProp` caching; thread-local error-handler stack, `std::mutex`, lock-free `NCollection_IncAllocator`; `IntCurveSurface` and `HLRBRep` share polyhedron code; mesh plugin factory; `BRepMesh_Delaun` CCW point-in-polygon fix; wire fixing no longer quadratic; STEP duplicate-entity removal (~20% smaller files); concurrent fillet construction fixes (`IntersectMoreCorner` crash, `MakeFillet::Add` hang, ellipse approach); "IGES read is not safe under concurrent use".
  - FOSDEM 2026 talk "OCCT3D 8.0: Evolving the Open Source Geometry Kernel" exists ([schedule](https://fosdem.org/schedule/event/QQRAAF-occt3d-8-kernel-evolution)) but the page returned 404/redirect loops; abstract not read.
- Known limitations: fillet faces are approximations; ChFi3d has no algorithm documentation; booleans require non-self-interfering inputs and cannot guarantee open solids; the cadrs README records OCCT fillets as 9–10× faster than cadrs' and OCCT booleans ~2–6× slower.
- Relevance to cadrs: `boolean`/`split`/`classify`/`unify` (the interference order, shrunk ranges, nested PaveFiller for new vertices, Fuzzy and Glue as named options rather than retries, Cells Builder as the model for `Kernel::cells`), `tol` (vertex tolerance rules), `tess` (the healer's parametric-space self-intersection loop; per-surface node splitters), `exchange`/`xbuild` (ShapeFix order and MaxTolerance cap), performance (8.0's devirtualised evaluation, by-value B-spline storage and `ExtremaPC` are the same levers as cadrs' allocation-free `nurbs`; the `kernel_perf.py` baseline is OCCT 7.8, so 8.0.1 may have moved it).

#### 1.2 truck (RICOS, Rust)
- URLs: https://github.com/ricosjp/truck ; shapeops docs https://docs.rs/truck-shapeops/latest/truck_shapeops/ ; source https://github.com/ricosjp/truck/tree/master/truck-shapeops/src
- Licence: Apache-2.0 — [README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ricosjp/truck/master/README.md).
- Approach: crates `truck-geometry` (knot vectors, B-splines, NURBS — the README says truck "re-implements the B-rep with NURBS"; no separate analytic surface types are mentioned), `truck-topology`, `truck-modeling`, `truck-shapeops` (booleans, healing, a fillet), `truck-meshalgo`, wgpu rendering — [README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ricosjp/truck/master/README.md). The boolean (`and`/`or`, signature `fn and<C: ShapeOpsCurve<S>, S: ShapeOpsSurface>(solid0, solid1, tol: f64) -> Option<Solid>`) **triangulates both shells with `tol`**, extracts the interference of the two polygon meshes, chains it into polylines, refines each polyline vertex onto both surfaces by `search_triple` (up to 100 trials; the solver is in another crate), divides faces in uv by signed-area outer/hole loops, classifies pieces by status inheritance along shared edges and resolves the remaining "Unknown" components by a signed-crossing count of one ray against the other shell's *mesh*, then converts the polyline intersection curves to B-splines by `BSplineCurve::quadratic_approximation` — [integrate](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ricosjp/truck/master/truck-shapeops/src/transversal/integrate/mod.rs), [intersection_curve](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ricosjp/truck/master/truck-shapeops/src/transversal/intersection_curve/mod.rs), [faces_classification](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ricosjp/truck/master/truck-shapeops/src/transversal/faces_classification/mod.rs), [divide_face](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ricosjp/truck/master/truck-shapeops/src/transversal/divide_face/mod.rs). The fillet is a single-edge rolling-ball surface (`RbfSurface`, `approx_rolling_ball_fillet`) with cubic Bézier side p-curves; the multi-edge version is a commented-out prototype — [fillet](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ricosjp/truck/master/truck-shapeops/src/fillet/mod.rs).
- Limitations (own docs): "Boolean operations are currently supported only for shapes where faces intersect transversally"; tangent faces unsupported; BSP acceleration listed as future work; fillets limited to one edge whose ends touch three faces — [lib.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ricosjp/truck/master/truck-shapeops/src/lib.rs). No tolerance model, no coincident-face handling, intersection curves are polylines refitted at the end.
- Relevance to cadrs: little to reuse; a useful *negative* reference for `ssi` and `boolean` (mesh-seeded intersections miss tangencies and small features; cadrs' exact analytic cases and signed-distance contouring are already ahead). Its `quadratic_approximation` curve fit and wgpu rendering are the only cross-over points.

#### 1.3 Fornjot (Hanno Braun, Rust) and ruststep (RICOS)
- URLs: https://github.com/hannobraun/fornjot ; https://github.com/ricosjp/ruststep
- Licence: Fornjot 0BSD; ruststep Apache-2.0.
- Fornjot: "This project has been shut down. Its goals were never reached." Archived 19 June 2026; the mainline had not been developed for over a year in favour of `experiments/`; "usable for simple models … lacks the features for anything more advanced" — [README](https://github.com/hannobraun/fornjot). No booleans or curved geometry documented.
- ruststep: `espr` EXPRESS-to-Rust compiler and an ASCII STEP deserialiser (v0.1.0, "experimental stage. DO NOT USE FOR PRODUCT"); writing, EXPRESS rules and AP-specific support on the roadmap — [README](https://github.com/ricosjp/ruststep).
- Relevance to cadrs: none for the kernel; `step` already reads and writes AP214. ruststep's `espr` could regenerate entity structs if cadrs ever adds AP242 kinematics/PMI.

#### 1.4 SolveSpace (GPL v3+)
- URLs: https://github.com/solvespace/solvespace ; `src/srf/surface.h`, `boolean.cpp`, `surfinter.cpp`
- Licence: GPL v3 or later — [README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/solvespace/solvespace/master/README.md).
- Approach (from source; no design doc exists): solids are shells of **rational Bézier patches with 4×4 control nets (degree ≤ 3 in each direction)**, trimmed by `STrimBy` references to `SCurve`s that are either exact `SBezier` (degree 1–3) or piecewise-linear — [surface.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/solvespace/solvespace/master/src/srf/surface.h). Booleans (`MakeFromBoolean`): build uv BSP classifiers for both shells (`SBspUv`, coordinates scaled by local tangents so tests are effectively in XYZ), split each shell's curves against the other, generate intersection curves per surface pair, drop short segments, rebuild the BSPs, trim each surface against the other shell, rewrite handles — [boolean.cpp](https://github.com/solvespace/solvespace/blob/master/src/srf/boolean.cpp). SSI handles **plane–plane, plane–extrusion (projected profile, or lines along the extrusion when parallel), and two parallel-axis extrusions exactly**; everything else is marched: seeds come from intersecting each surface's trim edges with the other surface, the march steps along n1 × n2 with the step adapted to keep the chord error between ½ and 1× `ChordTolMm`, each point projected back onto both surfaces, stopping at another seed or `max(300, 3·GetMaxSegments())` steps — [surfinter.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/solvespace/solvespace/master/src/srf/surfinter.cpp). Classification probes a chain midpoint pushed back onto the exact curve, probing distance capped at a tenth of the edge length; coincident faces are tagged COINC_SAME / COINC_OPP and each operation keeps a fixed combination. Grazing tangencies (issue #1291) are handled by exact trim curves because the numeric solve "can't converge on such a grazing intersection"; near-duplicate exact and numeric curves are filtered because "the duplicates would break the trim polygon assembly"; only real trim endpoints are used as split points because padded bounds produced phantom T-junctions (issue #1452) — [boolean.cpp](https://github.com/solvespace/solvespace/blob/master/src/srf/boolean.cpp).
- Limitations: degree ≤ 3, no NURBS (Bézier only), marched curves are polylines, failure sets a `booleanFailed` flag with no recovery, comments call the vertex-splitting approach "not clear [to be] the best method".
- Relevance to cadrs: `ssi` and `boolean` design sanity check — a second small kernel that found the same two rules cadrs uses (exact curves wherever the pair allows, seeds from trim-edge hits), and a concrete list of the failure modes (grazing tangency, duplicate curves, phantom split points) to keep in the conformance suite.

#### 1.5 SMLib via NVIDIA USD BRep (Apache-2.0)
- URLs: https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/usd-brep ; docs https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/usd-brep/latest/ ; Python API https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/usd-brep/latest/docs/python.html ; known issues https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/usd-brep/main/KNOWN_ISSUES.md ; legacy SMLib manual https://docs.nvidia.com/smlib/manual/smlib/introduction/index.html
- Licence: Apache-2.0, copyright 2020–2026 NVIDIA; the repo has 3 commits, ~1.8k stars, "currently not accepting contributions", no releases — [GitHub](https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/usd-brep). The legacy SMLib (Solid Modeling Solutions, built on Piegl & Tiller's NLib) is "limited to legacy customers; no new licenses" — [SMLib manual](https://docs.nvidia.com/smlib/manual/smlib/introduction/index.html). (Secondary reports of the Apache release: [AlphaSignal](https://alphasignal.ai/news/nvidia-opens-usd-brep-s-cad-kernel-to-developers-under-apache-2-0).)
- Documented capabilities (API level; no algorithm narrative anywhere): booleans union/difference/intersection/merge (merge keeps all faces, "Where the bodies touch, the result is non-manifold"), `boolean_with_options(keep_other_brep, cookie_cutter, imprinting, post_process, imprint_and_classify)`, boolean trees/lists, booleans with curves, 2D booleans; fillets `circular_fillet` / `chamfer_fillet` on all edges, `fillet_edges(edges, radius, xsect_type = linear|circular|blend, continuity = G1|G2|G3, thumbweight)` with per-edge radii, `variable_radius_fillet`, `SmApiSurfaceSurfaceFillet`, bevel corners, `remove_fillet`; `offset_brep(distance, extended_offset, self_intersection)` (distance must stay below the minimum concave radius of curvature), shell, curve/surface/profile offsets; linear, rotational, draft, curve and pipe sweeps, non-manifold sweeps with stitching, skins and ruled surfaces; tessellation; `compute_volume(relative_accuracy 1e-1..1e-8)` with closed forms for analytic primitives; `heal_brep` (tolerances adjusted to measured gaps, coincident vertices merged, degeneracies removed, periodic seams repaired, region classification fixed; "general geometric gap closing and endpoint refitting" not implemented); stitch variants; OCCT ASCII/binary import/export modules — [docs index](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/usd-brep/latest/), [Python API](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/usd-brep/latest/docs/python.html).
- Known issues (vendor list): `remove_fillet` does nothing but reports success; `heal_brep` can report success after a failed repair; seam handling on tori/spheres/cylinders from other systems (missing seams undetected, first seam on an uncut torus splits it into two faces, sphere bounded by coincident seams deleted as collapsed, edges through poles not split); inside-out triangles next to `circular_fillet` faces (workaround `surface_angle_tolerance_deg = 15`); mass properties off by ~1.4% at the default `relative_accuracy` 1e-3 on faces whose trims have interior knots (rational circles); malformed BrepArrays crash import — [KNOWN_ISSUES](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/usd-brep/main/KNOWN_ISSUES.md).
- Relevance to cadrs: `fillet` (cadrs already implements "SMLib's fillet executive"; the Apache source is now the reference implementation to compare corner, blend-section (G2/G3 "thumbweight") and `remove_fillet` behaviour against), `local` (`replace_faces` is "SMLib's local operation"), `boolean` (`imprint_and_classify` and `cookie_cutter` as named modes), `mass` (their accuracy bug on rational-circle trims is exactly the case cadrs' Green's-theorem integration must get right), `xbuild` (their seam/pole issue list is a healing checklist). The SMLib fillet, boolean and offset cases are already in `kernel_perf.py`.

#### 1.6 CGAL (Nef polyhedra, mesh booleans, algebraic kernel)
- URLs: Nef_3 https://doc.cgal.org/latest/Nef_3/index.html ; PMP booleans https://doc.cgal.org/latest/PMP_Boolean_operations/index.html ; package licences https://doc.cgal.org/latest/Manual/packages.html ; licence page https://www.cgal.org/license.html
- Licence: kernels and support LGPL v3+; most algorithms GPL v3+ (Nef_3 GPL, Polygon Mesh Processing GPL, Boolean Operations on Meshes GPL, 2D Arrangements GPL, 2D/3D circular/spherical kernels GPL, Algebraic Kernel LGPL); commercial licences from GeometryFactory — [packages](https://doc.cgal.org/latest/Manual/packages.html), [license](https://www.cgal.org/license.html).
- Nef_3: a Selective Nef Complex — every vertex carries a sphere map (its neighbourhood on a small sphere as a 2D Nef polyhedron with selection marks), edges stored twice, facets as boundary cycles, volumes as outer shell plus inner shells; closed under all Boolean set operations, complement, interior/closure/boundary/regularisation, non-manifold and mixed-dimensional; unbounded sets clipped to an infimaximal box and need extended kernels; exact arithmetic (`Exact_integer`, homogeneous kernel fastest with `leda_integer`; the filtered EPECK is faster in non-degenerate cases with `SNC_indexed_items`) — [Nef_3](https://doc.cgal.org/latest/Nef_3/index.html). Mesh booleans (moved to their own package in CGAL 6.2): *corefinement* — intersect triangle pairs with exact predicates, insert the intersection polylines as edges of both meshes, then classify; coplanar triangles get identical sub-triangulations; inputs must be closed, oriented, non-self-intersecting manifolds and the output must be manifold (union of two cubes sharing an edge fails; a `Non_manifold_output_visitor` gives a soup); with inexact constructions the output "may feature self-intersections", so EPECK or stored exact points are recommended for chained operations; `autorefine_triangle_soup` with iterative snap rounding (Lazard & Valque 2025) left 0 of 3,870 self-intersecting Thingi10k inputs self-intersecting versus 529 for naive rounding — [PMP booleans](https://doc.cgal.org/latest/PMP_Boolean_operations/index.html).
- Limitations: polyhedral only; the 3D circular/spherical kernels (2008–2010) never grew into curved-solid booleans; nothing on NURBS.
- Relevance to cadrs: `cells` and `boolean` on the planar subset (Nef's sphere maps are the exact way to represent non-manifold vertices if cadrs ever needs non-manifold results), `meshsolid` (autorefine + snap rounding before mesh-to-solid), `tol` (the EPICK/EPECK distinction — exact predicates, inexact constructions — is the vocabulary for cadrs' "decide exactly, construct to tolerance").

#### 1.7 libfive (Matthew Keeter) and implicit kernels
- URLs: https://libfive.com/ ; https://github.com/libfive/libfive ; Keeter, M. (2020) "Massively Parallel Rendering of Complex Closed-Form Implicit Surfaces", ACM TOG 39(4) — PDF https://www.mattkeeter.com/research/mpr/keeter_mpr20.pdf ; slides "Implicit kernels for solid modeling" (2018) https://www.mattkeeter.com/research/kernels.pdf
- Local: `Keeter-2020-massively-parallel-rendering-implicit-surfaces.pdf`, `Keeter-2018-implicit-kernels-for-solid-modeling-slides.pdf`
- Licence: libfive, stdlib and Python bindings MPL 2.0; Guile bindings and Studio GPL 2+ — [GitHub](https://github.com/libfive/libfive).
- Approach: solids are closed-form expression trees (functional representation); the meshing produces "watertight, manifold, hierarchical, feature-preserving triangle meshes" (the README names no algorithm) — [GitHub](https://github.com/libfive/libfive). The MPR paper evaluates the expression over a shallow spatial hierarchy with a high branching factor on the GPU; interval arithmetic both prunes empty regions and *simplifies the expression per region* (two orders of magnitude fewer operations in one benchmark); only C0 continuity is required, so Lipschitz-breaking warps and blends work — [search summary of the paper](https://www.mattkeeter.com/research/mpr/keeter_mpr20.pdf). - Limitations: no exact edges or faces; dimensions are resolution-limited; no B-rep output; GPU memory scaling is what Zoo cites for rejecting SDFs (entry 2.2).
- Relevance to cadrs: the GPU spike (per-region expression simplification under interval arithmetic is a technique for cadrs' GPU classification/sampling kernels: evaluate a signed distance over cells and prune); `offset_round` (an implicit offset is the trivial baseline cadrs' exact rounded offsets compete with). Skip as a kernel model.

#### 1.8 BRL-CAD (NURBS booleans on OpenNURBS)
- URLs: dev guide https://brlcad.org/docs/cad/devguides/en/bool_eval_development.html ; API https://brlcad.org/docs/api/d6/d5d/group__brep__boolean.html ; https://github.com/BRL-CAD/brlcad
- Licence: BRL-CAD libraries are LGPL 2.1 and applications BSD (from memory; the wiki licence page and the COPYING file were unreachable in this session, so **not re-verified**). - Approach: `ON_Boolean` (union/intersect/difference/XOR) on trimmed-NURBS `ON_Brep`s following Krishnan et al. 1995 (BOOLE). ### Inferences
- The only open kernels with a *documented* tolerance model are OCCT (per-entity tolerances, Fuzzy) and, by inheritance, SMLib (`heal_brep` "adjusting tolerances to measured gaps"). cadrs' three-level model plus retries is already more explicit than either; what it lacks is OCCT's named Glue/Fuzzy modes and SMLib's `imprint_and_classify`/`cookie_cutter` options as caller-selectable modes.
- OCCT 8.0's measurable changes are evaluation-path devirtualisation and container by-value storage, not new geometry; cadrs should re-run `kernel_perf.py` against 8.0.1 before quoting the 7.8 ratios.
- Every open Rust kernel other than cadrs either seeds intersections from meshes (truck) or stopped (Fornjot); there is no Rust precedent for exact analytic SSI or local fillets.

- ChFi3d's actual blend-surface construction and corner logic are undocumented beyond headers; reading `ChFi3d_Builder_0.cxx` / `ChFi3d_FilBuilder_C*.cxx` is the only way (left to the blends researcher).
- FOSDEM 2026 OCCT 8.0 talk abstract/slides not retrieved.
- BRL-CAD licence not re-verified (site maintenance); SMLib's internal boolean/fillet algorithms not described in any open doc; truck's `search_triple` solver not read.

## Key question 2: Commercial kernels — public technical material

### Takeaway
Vendors publish blend *configurations* and threading *modes*, not algorithms. The only primary algorithmic sources are Jackson 1995 (Parasolid tolerant modelling), the Zoo engine overview and Rockwood patent (GPU SSI by dense sampling), a handful of Siemens/Geometric patents on blend recognition, notch blends and blend removal, and C3D Labs' 2025–2026 posts. GPU use for geometry is claimed only by Zoo and (in 2013, OpenCL) RGK; Parasolid, ACIS, CGM and C3D are CPU-threaded.

### Cited Findings (entries)

#### 2.1 [MUST READ] Jackson, D. J. (1995). "Boundary representation modelling with local tolerances." ACM Solid Modeling '95. DOI 10.1145/218013.218072
- URL (author/institution mirror): https://ftp1.cs.wisc.edu/pub/users/prem/jackson-SM-95.pdf — Local: `Jackson-1995-Tolerant-modelling-Parasolid.pdf`
- Seminal (pre-2015) but the only primary account of Parasolid's tolerant modelling. Replaces a single global tolerance (Parasolid's 5e-9 m) with tolerances on vertices, edges and faces: a vertex region is a sphere; a **tolerant edge has no single 3D curve but a bundle of p-curves, one per adjoining face, all inside a tube of the edge's tolerance around one fixed p-curve**; faces keep the default tolerance. Validity rules are restated under tolerance: p-curve ends inside the vertex sphere; edges may only "intersect" (tolerance tubes overlap) in components contractible onto a common vertex, faces only in components contractible onto common edges/vertices; vertex tol ≥ edge tol ≥ face tol. Intersection and coincidence use the *sum* of tolerances. In booleans, ambiguous intersections raise tolerances *locally* and merge topology instead of raising a global tolerance. The paper argues why exact arithmetic (blow-up, spurious tiny features), perturbation (destroys intentional coincidence) and symbolic reasoning were rejected, and shows imported trimmed surfaces that meet only to the source tolerance becoming tolerant edges instead of forcing surface extension and re-intersection.
- Limitations: principles, not the full boolean; no performance data.
- Relevance to cadrs: `tol`, `topo`, `boolean`. cadrs carries per-vertex/edge tolerances "as OCCT"; Parasolid's distinct ideas are the p-curve-only tolerant edge and the contractibility rules (which would make `Body::check`'s `LOOP_GAP` rule precise), and local raise-and-merge as a targeted alternative to the whole-boolean retry at 1e-5/1e-4.

#### 2.2 [MUST READ] Zoo (KittyCAD) "Zoo CAD Engine Overview" (Farrell & Rockwood, 20 May 2025) and US patent 12,229,885 B1 (Rockwood, filed 13 May 2024, granted 18 Feb 2025)
- URLs: https://zoo.dev/research/zoo-cad-engine-overview ; https://patents.google.com/patent/US12229885B1/en ; USPTO PDF https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12229885 — Local: `Rockwood-2025-US12229885-Surface-to-surface-intersections-point-cloud-patent.pdf`
- Licence: engine proprietary (only the Design Studio app, github.com/kittycad/modeling-app, is open); patent public. The overview says "A patent for this method was recently allowed … application No. 18/662056", which is this patent (assignee the individual inventor).
- Approach: a B-rep kernel keeping NURBS "all throughout the stack", with a deliberately minimal set of surface primitives; fillets are "B-splines with specific constraints" rather than special blend surfaces. SSI is the GPU showcase: densely sample both surfaces on the GPU (e.g. 400×400), keep point pairs closer than a tolerance (embarrassingly parallel), a coarse 40×40 pass refined by 20×20 sub-samples near hits (effective 800×800), optional quad/octree culling; the kept points form a thick band in the master surface's uv space, thinned by a medial-axis-transform convolution, smoothed by a second ("hat") convolution, joints/endpoints classified via an adjacency matrix, ordered by nearest-neighbour walking, fitted by a B-spline and mapped back to 3D. The overview admits SSI "depends critically on the sampling", that convolution parameters need tuning and that averaging the two surfaces' results is untested. Sweeps: S(u,v) = T(v) + M(v)C(u), trajectories split into lines and arcs swept as extrusions/revolutions, curvature capped against self-intersection. SDF/implicit was rejected for GPU memory scaling. The v1 blog (21 May 2025) says SSI was "reformulated as a parallelizable root-finding problem", booleans are GPU-accelerated, dependencies include OpenNURBS and Vulkan, and "We'll publish detailed benchmarks as further optimizations land"; the KCL 3 post (8 Oct 2026) only reports booleans and fillets failing less often.
- Limitations: no benchmarks as of Oct 2026; sampling-based SSI cannot certify tangential or tiny intersections; cloud-only, streams video; boolean and fillet algorithms not described; "cannot handle this 3D subtraction yet" errors still acknowledged in 2026.
- Relevance to cadrs: `ssi`, GPU spike. cadrs' numeric SSI already contours a signed distance over a uv box; Zoo's is the GPU brute-force variant. Worth studying for the two-level sampling and uv-band thinning/ordering, and as a cautionary tale: a GPU SSI did not make booleans robust. Also the opposite design choice to cadrs' exact rolling-ball fillet strips.

#### 2.3 [MUST READ] C3D Labs posts: "C3D Geometric Kernel: New Features and Development Trends" (Kondrikova, 18 Sep 2025); "C3D Modeler Geometric Kernel: New Features" (19 Oct 2026); "Multithreading? Yes!" (Mitina, 5 Aug 2025); "General Spline Midsurfaces" (Patrushev, 10 Oct 2025); 2016 Intel/OpenMP post
- URLs: https://c3dlabs.com/blog/products/c3d-geometric-kernel-new-features-and-development-trends/ ; https://c3dlabs.com/blog/products/c3d-modeler-geometric-kernel-new-features/ ; https://c3dlabs.com/blog/products/multithreading-yes-how-to-use-the-c3d-kernel-in-multithreaded-applications/ ; https://c3dlabs.com/blog/tech-tips/general-spline-midsurfaces-in-the-c3d-geometric-kernel/ ; https://c3dlabs.com/blog/tech-tips/c3d-labs-works-with-intel-to-improve-its-3d-software/ ; public Doxygen (Russian) https://c3d.ascon.ru/doc/math/ — Local: `C3DLabs-2024-brochure.pdf` (feature list only)
- Licence: proprietary (free academic licences).
- Approach: booleans — the user sets the boolean's error; overlap is detected when the configured precision exceeds the segment distance; "controlled accuracy" being extended to more operations; tolerant geometry in boolean, extrude, revolve, sweep; **tolerant wireframe vertices**: a tolerant edge "leaves gaps in intersection curves" that keep connectivity through a tolerant vertex; shell trimming accepts gaps within a user accuracy. Fillets — both **rolling-ball** (path curves are loci of contact points, no directrix) and **rolling-disk** (directrix + normal section planes); multi-face fillets with circular or conic sections and three radius definitions (boundary curves, tangency to a third face group, a radius law); fillets between faces sharing no edge or in different shells; face-fillet flow "create fillets, then trim and stitch"; a procedure for self-intersecting fillets "detects the problem area, removes it, and patches it"; variable-radius fillets still in development (2026). Shells/offsets — midshell between any two face sets (sphere touching a reference-mesh node and the opposite set, centre = midsurface point, filter, re-mesh, adaptive spline fit, reported deviation); surface extension with three output modes. Diagnostics — shell self-intersection detector (faces-only / between faces / auto, optionally via triangulation), **operation logging that dumps inputs as JSON + C3D files before execution** (the `CADRS_DUMP_DIR` idea). Threading — OpenMP inside the kernel; modes Off/Standard/SaveItems/Items/Max, default maximum; per-thread lock-free caches disabled by default, enabled before and synchronised/garbage-collected after a parallel region (`EnterParallelRegion`/`ExitParallelRegion`, `ParallelRegionGuard`); parallel in plane projection, mass properties, triangulation, shell/surface/curve operations, converters, file I/O, thin walls, self-intersection detection, NURBS copy; topology objects are query-only across user threads. No GPU use.
- Limitations: no numbers anywhere; feature lists, not algorithm papers.
- Relevance to cadrs: `boolean` (user-settable boolean tolerance vs fixed retries), `fillet`/`blend` (rolling-disk vs rolling-ball; self-intersecting fillet patching; create-then-trim-and-stitch face fillets), `surfacing` (midsurface), `rebuild::task`/`tess` (which operations proved worth parallelising; per-thread caches), kernel debugging.

#### 2.6 Siemens blend patents: US 8,935,130 B2 "Notch blends in BRep models" (Mawby, Yu, Mao, Gao; filed 2011, granted 2015) and US 8,326,583 B2 "Blend recognition and chain sorting in a Brep model" (Yu, Xia, Lin, Mawby; 2012, lapsed 2025)
- URLs: https://patents.google.com/patent/US8935130B2/en ; https://patents.google.com/patent/US8326583B2/en — Local: `Siemens-2015-US8935130-Notch-blends-in-BRep-models-patent.pdf`, `Siemens-2012-US8326583-Blend-recognition-chain-sorting-patent.pdf`
- US8326583: recognises rolling-ball blends in imported models — a face is a candidate if its cross-section is a constant-radius circle, graded "accurate" (within a minimum tolerance), "tolerant" (within a maximum) or "blend-like"; tolerance absolute or relative to face size; candidates grouped by radius, connected sets form chains, dependent blends merged, blend-like faces adopt their accurate neighbours' radius; corners classified triangle / triangle-like / variable-radius / four-sided; each candidate is replaced by an exact blend and the chain re-blended. #### 2.7 US 7,236,168 B2 "Method for removing blends in B-rep models" (Venkataraman, Sohoni; Geometric Software Solutions, filed 2004, granted 2007, expired 2025)
- URL: https://patents.google.com/patent/US7236168B2/en — Local: `Venkataraman-2007-US7236168-Removing-blends-in-Brep-models-patent.pdf`

### Inferences
- The industry's tolerant-modelling story has not been re-published since 1995; the live variants are observable only through API options (C3D's per-operation precision, SMLib's `heal_brep`, CGM's "improve tolerances"). cadrs' design freedom here is real: nothing newer is known to be better, only untested.
- Blend robustness in shipping kernels comes from a long configuration catalogue (2.3, 2.4, 2.8) plus diagnostics (overflow, capping, interference), not from a different core construction; cadrs' local-trim rolling ball plus `blend` fallback matches the C3D "create, trim, stitch, patch self-intersections" flow.
- GPU use in commercial kernels is limited to sampling-style tasks (Zoo SSI, RGK mass/projection/picking) — the same boundary cadrs' spike drew (sampling, classification, quadrature on the GPU; topology on the CPU).

### Gaps
- Not searched before the budget ran out: Onshape engineering posts, PTC Granite, Autodesk ShapeManager papers, Dassault fillet patents 2018–2022, newer Siemens patents (2020–2025), COFES/CAD-developer talks, C3D papers in Programming and Computer Software / ISPRAS proceedings, Parasolid v35–v37 highlights, Spatial 2019–2025 release notes.
- Parasolid documentation (tolerant modelling chapter, blends) and ACIS/CGM technical articles are licensee-only; nTop whitepaper gated; Zoo has published no benchmarks.

## Key question 3: Recent academic work on kernel-level geometry

### Takeaway
No 2015–2026 paper does GPU booleans on curved B-reps; academic GPU work is trimming/ray tracing of trimmed NURBS and containment. The strongest transferable ideas are (a) generalized winding numbers on exact trimmed NURBS for classification, (b) correctly-rounded interval arithmetic (NFG/TIGHT/MiSo) and Bernstein-coefficient certification for SSI, distance and tessellation validity, and (c) the mesh-boolean lineage (indirect predicates, unevaluated intersection points, cell labelling by winding number). ML is used only for representation/generation, not for geometry computation.

### Cited Findings (entries)

**A. GPU-accelerated SSI, trimming, tessellation, integrals**

- **A1. Sloup, J. & Havran, V. (2021). "Optimizing Ray Tracing of Trimmed NURBS Surfaces on the GPU." CGF 40(7) (Pacific Graphics), 161–172. **B. Watertight / implicit / winding-number B-rep approaches**

- **B1. [MUST READ] Spainhour, J. & Weiss, K. (2026). "Robust Containment Queries over Collections of Trimmed NURBS Surfaces via Generalized Winding Numbers." ACM TOG 45(3), Art. 26. DOI 10.1145/3797957. arXiv:2504.11435.** URL https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11435 ; OA PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.11435 ; Local `Spainhour-2025-Containment-trimmed-NURBS-winding-numbers.pdf`. Point containment for trimmed-NURBS B-reps that need not be watertight: the solid-angle integral per trimmed patch is rewritten by Stokes as a line integral along the trim curves (plus a correction when the query projects inside the patch), computed by adaptive Gaussian quadrature with recursive bisection to an absolute error (1e-6); per-curve nodes memoised across queries; near-boundary points detected as edge cases. 0.15 ms/query on a 28-patch surface of revolution, 1.29 ms/query on a 192-patch filleted box–sphere (serial, Ryzen 7 5700); in LLNL's BSD-licensed Axom (https://github.com/llnl/axom); per-query evaluation is embarrassingly parallel. Limitations: per-patch cost per query (no hierarchy yet), near-boundary queries expensive, fractional GWN in gaps needs a 0.5 threshold, classification only. Relevance: `classify` (back up exact ray parity when a retry still leaves an open shell), `boolean_kinds`, `distance`, GPU spike, classification of imported non-watertight STEP before healing.
- **B2. Spainhour, J., Gunderman, D. & Weiss, K. (2024). "Robust Containment Queries over Collections of Rational Parametric Curves via Generalized Winding Numbers." ACM TOG 43(4), Art. 99. Relevance: `profile` (sketch regions with gaps), `tess`/`uvdomain` (point-in-trimmed-uv against p-curves that do not close — the `LOOP_GAP` case), `split` in uv. - **B3. Urick, B., Marussig, B., Cohen, E., Crawford, R. H., Hughes, T. J. R. & Riesenfeld, R. F. (2019). **C. Validated / interval numerics and certified solvers**

- **C1. [MUST READ] Sichetti, F., Attene, M. & Puppo, E. (2025). "TIGHT Intervals for Provably Correct Geometric Computation." STAG 2025 (Eurographics Italian Chapter). DOI 10.2312/stag.20251319. CC-BY.** URL https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/stag20251319 ; OA PDF https://diglib.eg.org/bitstream/handle/10.2312/stag20251319/stag20251319.pdf ; Local `Sichetti-2025-TIGHT-intervals.pdf`. Wraps Attene's NFG library (https://github.com/MarcoAttene/NFG — vectorised, rounding-mode-free intervals) and adds correctly-rounded transcendental functions (exp, log, trig, atan2, hypot, hyperbolic) so every operation returns the tightest float interval containing the true value (monotone: round endpoints; non-monotone: enumerate interior extrema); results are machine-independent and do not fail when inputs are a few ulps from degenerate. ~5× faster than Filib++ on continuous collision and ~16× on torus–torus SSI, both posed in MiSo as "cover all solutions in [0,1]⁴ within tolerance" by interval subdivision; the SSI output is a conservative region guaranteed to contain the whole locus. Limitations: library paper; SSI demo is subdivision-only (no Newton contraction, no topology); C++. Relevance: `tol`, `ssi` (prove a uv box empty before contouring; catch missed tangential branches), `distance`, `silhouette`, `hlr` (certify Newton results). A Rust port of NFG-style intervals (next-up/next-down by bit tricks, no rounding-mode switches) is feasible.
- **C2. Sichetti, F., Puppo, E., Huang, Z., Attene, M., Zorin, D. & Panozzo, D. (2025). "MiSo: a DSL for robust and efficient solve and minimize problems." ACM TOG 44(4).** (Citation from C1; no OA copy located.) Python DSL generating interval-based C++ solvers for SOLVE (cover a solution set) and MINIMIZE (constrained global optimisation) with correctness guarantees. Relevance: `ssi`, `distance` (min/max distance is a MINIMIZE problem), `blend` fit checks.
- **C3. Sichetti, F., Huang, Z., Attene, M., Zorin, D., Puppo, E. & Panozzo, D. (2025). "High-Order Continuous Geometrical Validity." ACM TOG 44(5), Art. 171. DOI 10.1145/3745763. arXiv:2406.03756.** OA PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.03756 ; Local `Sichetti-2024-High-order-continuous-geometrical-validity.pdf`. Conservative test that the Jacobian of a polynomial map stays positive along a trajectory by adaptive Bézier refinement of the Jacobian determinant (Bernstein coefficients bound it) plus time bisection, with floating-point rounding accounted for. Polynomial maps only. Relevance: `tess` and `sweep::frames` (certify a uv→3D map or a sweep frame has no fold), `loft_build` (self-intersecting skins), `tol` (a model of "conservative in floating point").
- **C4. Mizrahi, J. & Elber, G. "Topologically Guaranteed Bivariate Solutions of Under-constrained Multivariate Piecewise Polynomial Systems." - **C5. Burr, M. & Byrd, M. (2023/2024). Relevance: `split` in uv (certified p-curve crossings), `silhouette` (n·w = 0 is a Plantinga–Vegter problem; the small-normal-variation termination test would certify cadrs' marching squares), `hlr` crossings.

**G. Tolerant modelling (post-2015)**

- **G1. Huang, H., Chen, L., Shen, E. & Wang, J. (2023). ### Inferences
- The academic frontier relevant to cadrs is *certification*, not speed: interval/Bernstein bounds for SSI, silhouettes, tessellation validity and min/max distance are all within reach of a pure-Rust kernel, and nothing comparable exists in OCCT or (publicly) the commercial kernels.
- GPU work in the literature confirms cadrs' spike boundary: sampling, trimming tests, containment and quadrature parallelise well; topology does not.
- The mesh-boolean lineage (Attene, Cherchi, Kobbelt groups) is the best source of *engineering* patterns for `split`/`classify`: implicit intersection vertices, exact-or-filtered decisions, cell-level labelling by winding number, round-at-the-end.

### Gaps
- No 2015–2026 academic paper on GPU booleans for curved B-reps; no certified-topology SSI for rational surfaces beyond the Elber subdivision line and the TIGHT/MiSo demo (SINTEF/Mourrain "certified intersection" work is pre-2015).
- No OA copies located for Urick 2019, Attene 2020, Gunderman 2020/2021, Zhu 2025, Zou 2024, Liu/Liu/Fu 2025, Martens & Bessmeltsev 2025, MiSo 2025, Nehring-Wirxel 2021; EMBER and Zhou 2016 author PDFs exist but their hosts were unreachable.

## Key question 4: Benchmarks and test corpora

### Takeaway
Public B-rep corpora are ML datasets (ABC, Fusion 360 Gallery, MFCAD); none is a boolean/fillet robustness benchmark, but ABC (STEP from Onshape/Parasolid, filtered to models that need no healing) and the Fusion 360 segmentation set (42,912 STEP files labelled by the feature that made each face, including fillets and chamfers) are directly usable as kernel-gate inputs. OCCT's own test data and SMLib's cases are already in `kernel_perf.py`; Spatial's 200-case boolean suite and Zoo's benchmarks are not public; Thingi10k is the mesh-world standard.

### Cited Findings
- **ABC: Koch, S. et al. (2019). "ABC: A Big CAD Model Dataset for Geometric Deep Learning." CVPR 2019. arXiv:1812.06216.** OA PDF https://cims.nyu.edu/gcl/papers/2019-ABC-Dataset.pdf ; CVF https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/html/Koch_ABC_A_Big_CAD_Model_Dataset_for_Geometric_Deep_Learning_CVPR_2019_paper.html ; Local `Koch-2019-ABC-big-CAD-model-dataset.pdf`. ~1M models from Onshape in STEP and Parasolid formats, explicit parametrised curves and surfaces; "models that only consist of a single primitive or models that require shape healing during the translation and meshing phase are also filtered out"; STEP readable with Open Cascade and Gmsh. Licence: per-model Onshape public-document terms (check the supplement). Relevance: `step`/`xbuild` import gate at scale; mass-property and tessellation regression inputs; NH-Rep used 10K of it.
- **Fusion 360 Gallery (Autodesk): reconstruction (8,625 sketch-and-extrude sequences, r1.0.1, 2.0 GB), segmentation (35,680 parts, s2.0.1 3.1 GB; extended STEP set 42,912 files, 483 MB, faces labelled by the operation that created them, e.g. Extrude, Fillet, Chamfer), assembly (8,251 assemblies / 154,468 parts) and joint (32,148 joints) subsets.** https://github.com/AutodeskAILab/Fusion360GalleryDataset ; papers Willis et al. (2021) TOG 40(4), arXiv:2010.02392 (Local `Willis-2021-Fusion360-Gallery-dataset.pdf`); Lambourne et al. (2021) "BRepNet", CVPR 2021, arXiv:2104.00706 (Local `Lambourne-2021-BRepNet-Fusion360-segmentation.pdf`); Willis et al. (2021) "JoinABLe", arXiv:2111.12772. B-rep ground truth as `.smt` (Autodesk kernel) and `.step`, meshes as `.obj` — [arXiv 2010.02392 via search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.02392). Licence: see the repo's LICENSE.md (not read). Relevance: the reconstruction sequences are replayable feature scripts (extrude-only) for a rebuild/boolean gate; the segmentation set gives fillet and chamfer faces to re-derive and compare in `fillet`/`blend`.
- **MFCAD (Cao et al., 2020, "Graph representation of 3D CAD models for machining feature recognition with deep learning"):** https://github.com/hducg/MFCAD — STEP files in `dataset/step`, machining-feature labels, MIT licence, scripts need pythonocc-core; size and MFCAD++ not stated on the page. Relevance: synthetic prismatic parts with many booleans; a cheap boolean regression corpus. MFCAD++ (Colligan et al. 2022) not verified (budget).
- **OCCT test data** — the OCCT `tests/` scripts (boolean, blend, offset, pipe, draft, bugs) are the source of the 648-case `kernel_perf.py` run already in the cadrs README; OCCT 7.9.3 and 8.0.1 fixed new fillet/boolean regression cases (#738, #743, #859, #860; periodic-B-spline section hang) that are candidates to add — [7.9.3 via search](https://dev.opencascade.org/content/open-cascade-technology-793-released), [8.0 release](https://github.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/releases/tag/V8_0_0).
- **SMLib cases** — in `kernel_perf.py` already; the usd-brep KNOWN_ISSUES list (1.5) is a free set of healing/tessellation/mass-property edge cases.
- **Thingi10k** — the mesh robustness standard used by D2, D3, E1, E2 and CGAL's autorefine (3,870 of 10K inputs self-intersect) — [CGAL PMP booleans](https://doc.cgal.org/latest/PMP_Boolean_operations/index.html). Relevance: `meshsolid` only.
- **Not public:** Spatial's ">200 challenging industry test cases" boolean suite (2.8); Zoo's promised benchmarks (2.2); Parasolid's quality white paper (licensees).

### Inferences
- A kernel gate built from ABC (import + mass + mesh at scale), Fusion 360 segmentation (fillet/chamfer re-derivation), MFCAD (boolean-heavy prismatic parts) and the OCCT/SMLib cases would cover every module in the README's operations table except sweeps along paths and HLR, which still need in-house cases.

### Gaps
- No public boolean- or fillet-robustness benchmark with expected results exists; every corpus gives inputs only.
- MFCAD++ and the ABC licence terms not verified.
