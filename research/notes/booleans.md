# Robust B-rep boolean algorithms and tolerant modelling (research notes for cadrs_kernel)

Scope: complete B-rep boolean pipelines, tolerant modelling, robust arithmetic, coincidence handling, 2015-2026 mesh-arrangement booleans, and performance. Compared against the cadrs pipeline described in `crates/cadrs_kernel/README.md` (pool vertices/edges → split at V-E, E-E, E-F, F-F intersections → imprint coincident faces → split faces in uv → classify pieces by exact ray parity with in/out propagation through connected groups → assemble shells → `unify` same-surface faces; retries at 1e-6 → 1e-5 → 1e-4 on an open shell; vertices and edges carry their own tolerance).

## Q1. Complete boolean pipelines: what stages do they share and how do they classify faces?

### Takeaway
Every published B-rep boolean is the same four-stage pipeline cadrs already has (intersect low-dimensional entities first, imprint/split, classify, assemble, then simplify), and the systems differ mainly in (a) what they classify (faces of one body against the other, cells of a global arrangement, or connected "orientation-invariant" components) and (b) how they classify (neighbourhood/orientation tests at the intersection edge, parity of a ray, or propagation of a winding-number vector across a cell graph). The strongest systems classify only one representative per connected component and propagate across the intersection edges; "on-face" pieces are handled as a third state with orientation (same-sense or opposite-sense), never as in/out.

### Cited Findings

**[Rank 2] OpenCASCADE Technology, "Boolean Operations" specification (Boolean Component: General Fuse, Boolean, Splitter, Section, Cells Builder).** Canonical URL: https://dev.opencascade.org/doc/overview/html/specification__boolean_operations.html (now redirects to https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/overview/html/specification__boolean_operations.html). Markdown source mirrored at https://git.rbts.co/OpenCascade/occt/raw/branch/IR/dox/specification/boolean_operations/boolean_operations.md. No PDF (HTML only, not downloaded).
Algorithm: the General Fuse Algorithm (GFA) is the base of Boolean, Splitter and Section operators; all share one data structure `BOPDS_DS` and an *Intersection Part* (`BOPAlgo_PaveFiller`) followed by a *Building Part* (`BOPAlgo_Builder` / `BOPAlgo_BOP` / `BOPAlgo_Splitter`), only the building part differing per operator. The intersection part runs in a fixed order over interference types, each step feeding the next: (1) initialisation, with `BOPDS_Iterator` finding candidate sub-shape pairs by bounding boxes; (2) Vertex/Vertex, where interfering vertices are grouped into connexity chains (`BOPAlgo_Tools::MakeBlocksCnx`) and each chain yields one new vertex at the centre of the sphere enclosing the source tolerance spheres; (3) Vertex/Edge, computing paves, the vertex tolerance becoming `Max(Tol(V), D + Tol(E))` where D is the vertex-to-curve distance, then splitting pave blocks at interior paves; (4) Edge/Edge via `IntTools_EdgeEdge`, a VERTEX common part producing new vertices that are re-intersected in a sub-PaveFiller, an EDGE common part producing coinciding pave-block chains that become *common blocks*; (5) Vertex/Face; (6) Edge/Face via `IntTools_EdgeFace`, vertex parts becoming new vertices, edge parts becoming common blocks on faces, `FaceInfo` (per-face In/On lists) updated; (7) split edges built from pave blocks (`BOPTools_AlgoTools::MakeSplitEdge`); (8) Face/Face via `IntTools_FaceFace` returning intersection curves and points each with a tolerance; (9) draft section edges intersected again in a sub-PaveFiller (`PostTreatFF`); (10) p-curves built for section edges and common blocks, degenerated edges processed. Vocabulary: a *pave* binds a vertex to a curve parameter; a *pave block* is the curve between neighbouring paves; a *common block* is a set of coincident pave blocks plus the faces they lie on; the *shrunk range* of a pave block is the part that can still interfere after subtracting the end tolerance spheres. The building part builds images bottom-up (vertices, edges, wires, faces, shells, solids, compsolids, compounds): `BOPAlgo_BuilderFace` makes split faces from split and section edges with orientation rules; *same-domain (SD) faces* are detected pairwise with `AreFacesSameDomain`, grouped into connexity chains (`myShapesSD`) and one representative kept; solids are built by `BOPAlgo_BuilderSolid` from faces In a solid, with same-domain solids mapped; post-processing `CorrectPointOnCurve` / `CorrectCurveOnSurface` adjusts tolerances so the result passes `BRepCheck_Analyzer`. Classification is by state In/On/Out relative to the opposite argument: Fuse keeps Out parts (internal faces removed for solids); Common keeps In or On; Cut12 keeps Out parts of S1; Cut21 Out parts of S2; for open-solid (shell) arguments: COMMON takes faces "inside any solid of the opposite group", FUSE "outside of all solids of the opposite group", CUT object faces outside all tools plus tool faces inside any object. Dimension rules: Fuse needs equal dimensions, Cut needs Dim(S2) ≥ Dim(S1), Common any. The Cells Builder takes a split part if it is IN all `ShapesToTake` and OUT of all `ShapesToAvoid`, assigns a material, and `RemoveInternalBoundaries` removes boundaries only between same-material cells. History: only edges and faces have Generated shapes; CUT of fully coincident faces deletes both, partial overlap keeps the object face. — [OCCT spec](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/overview/html/specification__boolean_operations.html)
Options (see Q2, Q4, Q6 for details): Fuzzy value, Gluing (Off/Shift/Full), `SetUseOBB`, `SetRunParallel`, `SetNonDestructive` (safe mode: no tolerance increase and no p-curves added to inputs, "might slightly decrease the performance"), `SetCheckInverted` (default on; off saves "up to 10 percent" but result may be invalid if negative volumes exist), BOP result simplification via `ShapeUpgrade_UnifySameDomain` with the fuzzy tolerance passed as its linear tolerance. — [OCCT spec](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/overview/html/specification__boolean_operations.html)
Input preconditions: arguments must be valid per `BRepCheck_Analyzer`, must not be self-interfered ("shapes that coincide geometrically must share topology"), faces/edges at least C1; the spec states `BRepCheck_Analyzer` validity is "necessary but insufficient". Errors vs warnings: `HasErrors()`, `HasWarnings()`, alert types in `BOPAlgo_Alerts.hxx` (e.g. `BOPAlgo_AlertTooSmallEdge`). — [OCCT spec](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/overview/html/specification__boolean_operations.html)
Limitations: no explicit full In/On/Out decision table in the spec; fuzzy propagation not documented beyond "sum of tolerances"; gluing is unchecked ("the algorithm does not check this itself", wrong use "is likely to lead to incorrect result").
Relevance to cadrs: `boolean` (the pave/pave-block/common-block structure is a cleaner model of cadrs' "pool of vertices and edges split at intersections" and gives a name to the coincident-piece merge: common blocks); `boolean_kinds` (OCCT's dimension rules and open-solid rules are a ready table to check cadrs' result-kind table against); `unify` (`UnifySameDomain` run with the fuzzy tolerance, and SD-face detection as a connexity chain, mirrors `merge_coplanar`); `tol` (the `Max(Tol(V), D + Tol(E))` rule and vertex-chain sphere centre are concrete tolerance-growth rules). The sub-PaveFiller re-intersection of new vertices/section edges (steps 4, 9) is the piece cadrs' single-pass split may be missing when it has to retry at a larger tolerance.

**[Rank 5] Hoffmann, C. M., Hopcroft, J. E., Karasick, M. S., "Robust set operations on polyhedral solids", IEEE Computer Graphics and Applications 9(6):50–59, 1989.** DOI https://doi.org/10.1109/38.41469. Open-access TR versions: Purdue CSD-TR-723 https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/cstech/624 and Cornell TR 87-875 https://hdl.handle.net/1813/6715; author PDF https://www.cs.purdue.edu/cgvlab/www/resources/papers/Hoffmann-IEEE-1989-Robust_set_operations_on_polyhedral_solids.pdf. Local: `../papers/booleans/Hoffmann-Hopcroft-Karasick-1989-robust-set-operations.md` (the Purdue TR, 38 pp.).
Algorithm: regularized operations are reduced to intersection and complement on a non-manifold "Star-Edge" representation whose only numerical data are face plane coefficients (vertices implicit as the intersection of three planes, auxiliary planes allowed for near-tangent faces). Shell-level outline: intersect every shell of A with every shell of B; merge intersecting shells; add shells of A inside B and of B inside A (containment by classifying a connecting line, after Requicha & Voelcker 1985). Shell merging is deliberately asymmetric: for each face f of A, intersect its plane P with B to get a *cross-sectional graph* G_P whose edges are B's edges in P and "cross-face edges" (segments of P ∩ faces of B); directed-edge orientation labels every area of P as inB, outB, oninB (B's surface in P with the same orientation as P) or onoutB (opposite orientation, "cannot be part of the surface of A ∩ B"); then intersect G_P with f's boundary cycles, mark directed edges of f inside inB areas and of G_P inside f, discard the rest, pair the remaining radially adjacent directed edges; faces of A with empty G_P are included if reachable by adjacency from a face with non-empty G_P (breadth-first closure); finally transfer the result's edges and isolated vertices to B's faces (five edge types; an edge coincident with an edge of A is transferred to the faces of B between volume-enclosing face pairs by merging the two radially-ordered directed-edge lists) and subdivide B's faces the same way. Robustness comes from incidence tests that use the per-computation uncertainty ε (points "distant" if separated by ε_a + ε_b, else "near") and resolve "near" cases symbolically from adjacent features (vertex-on-plane via intersections of incident edges with the plane; vertex-on-vertex tested asymmetrically only as "vertex of the first solid on each plane of a vertex of the other"), and from ordering/pairing operations that use radial order instead of coordinates when points are near. — [HHK 1989](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/cstech/624)
Results: two unit cubes rotated by a small angle — intersection correct down to about 1/200°, below that the cubes are deemed identical; a 3034-face "polyhedral sphere" built by 12 iterated intersections with a box preprocessor giving "performance close to linear" (O(n log² n + k) box-pair search); the authors note after an operation the result may have ε-edges and propose face tilting to remove them (unfinished). — [HHK 1989](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/cstech/624)
Limitations (stated): incidence tests designed for valence-3 vertices, "if two solids coincide at a vertex with high valence ... our intersection algorithm may fail"; no proof of consistency; polyhedra only; vertex coincidence not transitive (made safe only by a minimum feature separation in the inputs).
Relevance to cadrs: `classify` (the oninB/onoutB split — "on" pieces classified by whether the two faces' normals agree — is exactly the rule cadrs needs for imprinted coincident faces in union vs. subtract; the breadth-first closure over faces with no intersections is cadrs' "propagation through connected groups"); `boolean` (asymmetric processing: classify A's faces against B fully, then *transfer* the result edges to B's faces rather than re-intersecting B against A, so the same incidence question is never asked twice in two numerical forms); `topo` (radial ordering of faces about an edge and of edges about a vertex as the primitive for pairing, which cadrs needs for non-manifold results and for "kissing" edges).

**[Rank 7] Keyser, J., Culver, T., Foskey, M., Krishnan, S., Manocha, D., "ESOLID — A system for exact boundary evaluation", Proc. 7th ACM Symposium on Solid Modeling and Applications (SM '02), 2002 (journal version Computer-Aided Design 36(2):175–193, 2004).** Project page https://gamma.cs.unc.edu/ESOLID; PDF http://gamma.cs.unc.edu/ESOLID/keyser02.pdf. Local: `../papers/booleans/Keyser-2002-ESOLID-exact-boundary-evaluation.md`. Companion: **Krishnan, S., "Efficient and Accurate Boundary Evaluation Algorithms for Boolean Combinations of Sculptured Solids", PhD dissertation, UNC Chapel Hill, 1997** (the BOOLE system), PDF https://www.cs.unc.edu/xcms/wpfiles/dissertations/krishnan.pdf, local `../papers/booleans/Krishnan-1997-dissertation-boundary-evaluation-sculptured-solids.md`. Also **Foskey, Manocha, Culver, Keyser, Krishnan, "Reliable geometric computations with algebraic primitives and predicates", Workshop on Uncertainty in Geometric Computations 2001**, PDF http://gamma.cs.unc.edu/ESOLID/foskey02.pdf, local `.../Foskey-2001-reliable-algebraic-primitives-predicates.pdf`.
Algorithm (ESOLID): solids are B-reps of trimmed rational parametric patches with both parametric and implicit forms; intersection curves are stored implicitly as algebraic plane curves in each patch's uv domain (not parameterised), with endpoints as MAPC 2D intervals of rational bounds that are shrunk on demand. Stage 1, per patch pair: generate the intersection curve by substituting one patch's parametrisation into the other's implicit form; resolve its topology in the domain; intersect it with the trimming boundary and *invert* each point into the other patch's domain (reduced to 2D curve–curve intersections plus 3D interval matching, iterated until unambiguous); determine *curve correspondence* (orientation of the same 3D curve in the two domains) from the inverted points rather than by tracing; clip to both trimmed regions. Stage 2, per patch: merge patch/patch curves into patch/solid curves; partition the patch; classify partitions by classifying one contained point against the other solid; choose components per operation and update topology. — [Keyser 2002](http://gamma.cs.unc.edu/ESOLID/keyser02.pdf)
Krishnan 1997 (BOOLE, floating point) gives the culling and classification detail: patch pairs are pruned by axis-aligned boxes of control points (tightened by the trimming region's uv rectangle) reported by a Z-sweep with nested segment trees in O(n log² n + k), then by a Seidel linear-programming separating-plane test on the control polytopes (O(k)); intersection curves are merged within a patch only if "the two surfaces that generated these curves are adjacent in the other solid" (a topological test replacing a tolerance comparison); the refined face-adjacency graph splits each face node into its partitions, its connected components are "orientation invariant" (each lies wholly inside or wholly outside the other solid), so *only two ray-shooting operations per CSG operation* are needed (one per solid) and all other components follow by adjacency ("two adjacent components must have opposite classifications"); near-boundary 2D classification is made reliable by evaluating the sign of the trimming curve's defining polynomial via SVD; degeneracies such as face-face and edge-edge overlaps are "handled as special cases". — [Krishnan 1997](https://www.cs.unc.edu/xcms/wpfiles/dissertations/krishnan.pdf)
Results: ESOLID is "less than one order of magnitude slower in most cases and no more than two orders of magnitude slower in the worst case" than BOOLE; curve-curve intersection takes 54–98 % of the time (Table 3), dominated by resultant and Sturm-sequence computations; BOOLE fails on near-tangent cylinders ("curves did not close") and on an apparently singular (actually two-component) intersection curve where ESOLID succeeds. Precision needed for two cylinders barely interpenetrating rises from 20 to 141 bits as the penetration depth falls from 1e-3 to 1e-45. — [Keyser 2002](http://gamma.cs.unc.edu/ESOLID/keyser02.pdf)
Limitations: degree ≤ 4 surfaces practical; "Boolean operations are not supported for degenerate configurations ... surfaces of different objects should not overlap"; ESOLID "cannot be considered a robust system, in terms of handling all possible input configurations"; memory heavy.
Relevance to cadrs: `classify` (Krishnan's rule: classify one point per orientation-invariant component and flip across intersection edges, which is what cadrs' propagation does; the paper confirms two rays per boolean suffices and that the hard part is the near-boundary 2D point-in-trim test); `boolean` / `ssi` (curve correspondence via inverted points instead of tracing; merging curves by surface adjacency instead of endpoint tolerance; the Seidel separating-plane cull after boxes); `split` (the stack-based partition algorithm in §6.2.2 yields adjacency between partitions for free, avoiding later ray shots).

### Inferences
- cadrs' pipeline is structurally the Jackson/OCCT pipeline (imprint → join → select ≈ pool+split → imprint → classify+assemble). What the strongest systems add in the *intersection* stage is iteration: OCCT re-intersects new vertices and section edges in a sub-PaveFiller; Jackson re-compares split edges "since the smaller edges may now be coincident when they were not before"; Segal reruns a face pair when a merge changes tolerances. cadrs' retry-at-larger-tolerance is a coarse version of this; a local re-intersection of only the new entities would be the published fix.
- Every curved-solid system (BOOLE/ESOLID, OCCT) classifies by a point-in-solid test of one representative per connected piece plus propagation; the polyhedral systems (HHK, Mäntylä) classify by local neighbourhood orientation at the intersection edge. The cadrs ray-parity + propagation design matches the curved-solid lineage; what HHK adds is the explicit "on, same sense" / "on, opposite sense" state for coincident faces.
- OCCT's "shrunk range" (pave block minus end tolerance spheres) is the formal answer to "can a tiny split edge still interfere?" and is cheap to adopt.

## Q2. Tolerant modelling: how is a valid tolerant model defined and how is tolerance growth contained?

### Takeaway
Jackson (Parasolid) and Segal define validity the same way: each vertex/edge/face has a tolerance region (sphere / tube / slab), all tests use the *sum* of the two entities' tolerances, and the model is valid iff tolerance regions of entities that are not topologically adjacent do not intersect — more precisely, each connected component of the intersection of two entities' regions must contain (Jackson: be contractible onto) a common lower-dimensional entity. Both grow tolerances only by *merging* topology (vertex into vertex, short edge into a vertex, thin face into an edge), never by inflating an entity in place, and both admit they cannot bound the growth: Jackson lists "preventing tolerance growth" as future work and Segal shows iterated unions collapsing a cube to a single vertex after 11 iterations. OCCT's fuzzy value is a per-operation additive tolerance on top of the entity tolerances, used explicitly to turn near-coincident into coincident.

### Cited Findings

**[Rank 1] Jackson, D. J., "Boundary representation modelling with local tolerances", Proc. 3rd ACM Symposium on Solid Modeling and Applications (SMA '95), pp. 247–254, 1995.** DOI https://doi.org/10.1145/218013.218069. Open PDF (Wisconsin mirror): https://ftp1.cs.wisc.edu/pub/users/prem/jackson-SM-95.pdf. Local: `../papers/booleans/Jackson-1995-local-tolerances.md`.
Model: each vertex has a tolerance (default 5e-9 m), region a sphere; an edge with tolerance above default is a "tolerant edge" represented *not* by one 3-space curve but by its parameter-space curves on each adjacent face, with the tolerance tube centred on one (arbitrary but fixed) p-curve so all p-curves lie in the tube; faces have a thickness tolerance (always default in Parasolid at the time). Ordering rule: "The vertex tolerance must be at least as great as that of connected edges, and the edge tolerance must be at least as great as that of connected faces." Tolerances attach to topology, not geometry: points, curves, surfaces, point-on-surface and trimmed curves are treated as exact. — [Jackson 1995](https://ftp1.cs.wisc.edu/pub/users/prem/jackson-SM-95.pdf)
Validity: "two entities intersect if they approach within the sum of their tolerances"; ends of p-curves must lie inside the vertex sphere and "the curves bounding a face need not meet exactly end-to-end, as long as they are within vertex tolerance"; "edges must only intersect at their end vertices. If the intersection of the tolerance tubes of two edges is non-empty, then each connected component of that intersection must intersect a common vertex of the two edges"; faces likewise: each connected component of the intersection of two faces' regions "must be contractible onto a collection of common edges or vertices" (Fig. 4: a self-intersecting face is invalid, a face whose tube overlap is contractible to the vertex is valid). — [Jackson 1995](https://ftp1.cs.wisc.edu/pub/users/prem/jackson-SM-95.pdf)
Boolean (imprint / join / select): imprint compares low-dimensional topology first — (a) vertex-vertex ("one vertex may correspond to more than one on the other model"); (b) vertex-edge: split the edge, the new vertex inheriting the split edge's tolerance, noting the split "may result in removal of nearby topology, some of which may already have been matched"; (c) edge-edge with the sum of edge tolerances returning *areas of coincidence* (parametric ranges), ignoring those that contain a vertex (already found by vertex-edge); (d) vertex-face: add a corresponding vertex to the face; (e) edge-face: intervals of coincidence on the curve, "since the edge has already been compared with edges of the face, we need only consider coincidence, or intersections interior to the face and edge"; for coincidence add an edge to the face between two existing vertices; "if an edge is split by this process, the pieces need to be re-compared with other edges"; (f) face-face: the surface intersector "need not consider curves (i.e. contact areas) already known to be common by virtue of edge-edge or edge-face comparison. This can be important, since computation of near-tangent intersection curves can be very unstable"; trim the curves against both faces, add sections on both faces as corresponding edges, re-compare split edges (cheap since "only edges with known common vertices need be considered"). After imprint "each face lies inside, outside, or on, the boundary of the other model". *Join*: "when entities of different tolerances correspond, the join operation will use the larger of the two tolerances. This may result in removal of adjacent topology"; topology is compressed until there is a one-to-one correspondence of matched vertices, edges, faces, then the models are joined into one non-manifold cellular model. *Select*: keep by inside/outside classification per operation, optionally removing lower-dimensional topology, splitting disconnected results, or returning manifold sub-models. The paper's example: a thin face degenerating to an edge must be removed when two imprinted vertices coincide (Fig. 5); a vertex whose sphere contains a whole edge forces that edge's removal and its ends combined. — [Jackson 1995](https://ftp1.cs.wisc.edu/pub/users/prem/jackson-SM-95.pdf)
Sewing is "the part of the boolean algorithm which deals with matching edges and vertices; the face-face and edge-face comparisons are omitted"; a thin hole left by faces that do not meet is closed by raising edge tolerances and re-sewing. Future work: face tolerances to avoid "expensive and unreliable surface intersections" of approximately coincident or tangent faces; "the recursive compression of topology ... has not been fully implemented"; "Compression normally results in some growth of local tolerances ... this seems not to be achievable in all cases, tolerance growth should be contained where possible". — [Jackson 1995](https://ftp1.cs.wisc.edu/pub/users/prem/jackson-SM-95.pdf)
Limitations: polyhedral/curved agnostic but gives no algorithm for compression beyond examples; no bound on tolerance growth; face tolerances unimplemented.
Relevance to cadrs: `tol` (the ordering rule vertex ≥ edge ≥ face and the "sum of tolerances" test are already the cadrs model; the *validity* criterion — tolerance regions of non-adjacent entities must not overlap, and overlaps of adjacent ones must be connected and contain the shared entity — is the check `Body::check` should make for tolerant bodies); `topo` (representing a tolerant edge by its p-curves with the 3-space curve demoted is how Parasolid keeps p-curve/curve mismatch legal); `boolean` (the ordering of comparisons and especially *skipping face-face intersection where edge-edge/edge-face already found the contact* is a direct recipe for cadrs' tangent/near-coincident failures; "join uses the larger tolerance and removes adjacent topology" is the compression step cadrs' retry loop approximates globally).

**[Rank 4] Segal, M., "Using tolerances to guarantee valid polyhedral modeling results", Computer Graphics (Proc. SIGGRAPH '90) 24(4):105–114, 1990.** DOI https://doi.org/10.1145/97880.97891. Open PDF (Wisconsin mirror): https://ftp1.cs.wisc.edu/pub/users/prem/segal-repair-siggraph-1990.pdf; archive record https://history.siggraph.org/?p=122053. Local: `../papers/booleans/Segal-1990-tolerances-polyhedral.md`. Precursor: **Segal & Séquin, "Maintaining topology in geometric descriptions with numerical uncertainty", UC Berkeley TR UCB/CSD-88-450, 1988**, PDF http://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/1988/Archive/CSD-88-450.pdf, local `.../Segal-Sequin-1988-maintaining-topology-numerical-uncertainty.pdf` (scanned, no text layer).
Model: each feature f has a flat (point/line/plane), a tolerance ε, and tolerance region r = {q : dist(q, flat) < ε}; a feature's region tol(f) is the union of its boundary features' regions with the slab/tube over its own interior; edges and faces also carry an *extent* ext(f) (max deviation of the feature's region from its flat) and a *near* value near(f) = the smallest distance to any feature previously found *not* to intersect f. Two flats approximately intersect if their distance ≤ ε_i + ε_j; "consistency for inexact objects is the same as consistency for exact objects with intersection replaced by approximate intersection", and the modeler guarantees approximately consistent output from approximately consistent input. — [Segal 1990](https://ftp1.cs.wisc.edu/pub/users/prem/segal-repair-siggraph-1990.pdf)
Restrictions on feature alignments (what a valid tolerant model forbids): approximate alignment of equal-dimension features must be made symmetric and transitive by fiat; "equal-dimensional features never approximately coincide; such coincidence is represented with a single feature"; intersecting tolerance regions whose boundary regions intersect in more than one connected component are illegal (Fig. 5); two features aligned with a higher-dimensional feature whose projections also align must themselves be aligned (Fig. 6, the "ordering of vertices along an edge is not well defined" case). — [Segal 1990](https://ftp1.cs.wisc.edu/pub/users/prem/segal-repair-siggraph-1990.pdf)
Algorithm: face-pair partitioning (Laidlaw–Trumbore–Hughes style) with tolerance-aware steps: the intersection line of two planes gets a tolerance large enough that its region encloses tol(a1) ∩ tol(a2), ε = sqrt(ε1² + ε2² + 2ε1ε2 cos α)/sin α with sin α = |n1 × n2|; faces are tested for coplanarity when max(ε1, ε2)/… exceeds a user "coplanarity factor" n (typically 100–1000) and deemed coplanar if two vertices of one lie on opposite sides of the other's plane or any vertex is within the other's extent; a vertex lies on the intersection line if ||p − v||² − [u·(p − v)]² ≤ (ε + ε_p)²; signed distances update near values; intersection points are sorted along the line and adjacent points that approximately intersect are *merged* (new sphere = minimum enclosing sphere of the two; for edges, the enclosing bounded cylinder; for faces, Newell-plane averaging with the tolerance set to the max vertex deviation); merging triggers topological repair (eliminate the edge between merged vertices, merge edges with the same two vertices, collapse illegal two-edge contours, remove spikes); coplanar faces are not partitioned against each other directly but "we rely on there being two contours on either side of an edge ... This third face will induce partitions in each of the coplanar faces". *Backtracking*: if a merged feature's new tolerance exceeds its near value, a previous non-intersection decision may be wrong, so near is reset and "the algorithm is rerun from the beginning"; it terminates because backtracking only merges features. — [Segal 1990](https://ftp1.cs.wisc.edu/pub/users/prem/segal-repair-siggraph-1990.pdf)
Results and limits: two cubes rotated by 9.59863838340879299° vs ...300° (one mantissa bit apart) give the two expected topologies on either side; "the algorithm runs 5–10 times slower, even when backtracking is not required"; O(n²) in faces, O(n) average with spatial subdivision (not implemented); worst case O(e^f) with backtracking; an iterated union/rotation test keeps tolerances "imperceptibly small" for 8 iterations, then "features are rapidly collapsed so that after 11 iterations, the object is reduced to a single vertex"; the author suggests reporting large tolerances to the user and a relaxation post-process to shrink tolerances. — [Segal 1990](https://ftp1.cs.wisc.edu/pub/users/prem/segal-repair-siggraph-1990.pdf)
Relevance to cadrs: `tol` (the *near* value is the missing bookkeeping for cadrs' retry loop: instead of rerunning the whole boolean at 10× tolerance, record for each entity the smallest rejected distance and rerun only when a merge's new tolerance exceeds it; the minimum-enclosing-sphere rule for merged vertices is what OCCT's V/V step also does); `topo` (the explicit list of repairs after a merge — degenerate edge, duplicate edge between two vertices, two-edge loop, spike — is a checklist for cadrs' pool/merge); `split` (the sorted-points-along-the-intersection-line merge is the 1-D version of cadrs' edge splitting and shows where tolerant merges belong: *after* sorting, before cutting).

**Qi, J., Shapiro, V., Stewart, N. F., "Single-set and class-of-sets semantics for geometric models" (preprint title "Topological regularity in semantics of geometric models"), Computer-Aided Design 38(10):1088–1098, 2006.** DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cad.2006.05.002. Preprint PDF https://ftp1.cs.wisc.edu/pub/users/prem/topo-regularity.pdf; lab page https://spatial.engr.wisc.edu/2005-2001 (fetch returned 403). Local: `../papers/booleans/Qi-Shapiro-epsilon-regular-sets.md`.
Content: two semantics for an inaccurate B-rep Δ. *Single-set* (Quasi-NURBS, Andersson/Stewart et al.): perturb faces and boundary curves to a unique regular set S with S ⊨ Δ, with the adjustment "no larger than the largest discrepancy already existing in the face at its boundary" — supports classical backward error analysis but requires a combinatorially equivalent well-formed set to exist. *Class-of-sets* (Virtual-Interval, Qi & Shapiro): define the model by its point-membership classification Π_Δ and prove S− ⊆ S_in ⊆ S_out^c ⊆ S+; the interval [S−, S+] is *ε-regular* iff i_ε(S+) ⊆ S− and k_ε(S−) ⊇ S+ (ε-interior and ε-closure), which makes S+ \ S− a "thickened boundary that hides small imperfections" and bounds the Hausdorff distances between S−, S+, their complements and boundaries by ε; a practical S± is the union of δ-balls over the geometry G with δ the "maximum gap size" of the PMC algorithm. The class-of-sets semantics "does not require preservation of the specific combinatorial structure of the model's boundary". — [Qi, Shapiro, Stewart](https://ftp1.cs.wisc.edu/pub/users/prem/topo-regularity.pdf)
Limitations: a semantics, not an algorithm; the ε-regularity paper itself (Qi & Shapiro, "ε-regular sets and intervals", SMI 2005 / "ε-topological operations") was not found as an open PDF.
Relevance to cadrs: `tol` (gives the definition cadrs' `check` should aim at for tolerant bodies: the body is valid if every point further than ε from all faces classifies consistently, i.e. the thickened boundary of width = entity tolerance is the only ambiguous region; this is what justifies merging anything inside a tolerance tube); `classify` (a point classification must be made *outside* the thickened boundary — ray start points and test points must be chosen at distance > tolerance from all faces, which is exactly the failure mode of near-coincident classification).

**OCCT Fuzzy option** (same spec as Q1). "Additional tolerance for the operation", default `Precision::Confusion()`; the user measures the gap or embedding depth, increases it slightly and passes it in, so shifted entities "become coincident in terms of their tolerance plus the additional one"; interference is tested on the sum of tolerances. Documented examples: a cylinder offset by 5e-5 leaving a thin artifact; boxes 1e-6 apart leaving a narrow face; a 1e-5 gap leaving a pin; overlapping edges deviating by 5.28004e-5 where a fuzzy value of 6e-5 "gave a stated speedup of 45x". Non-destructive mode keeps inputs untouched ("no tolerance increase and no added P-Curves"). — [OCCT spec](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/overview/html/specification__boolean_operations.html)

**Huang, H. et al. (Tsinghua), "B-rep Boolean resulting model repair by correcting intersection edges based on inference procedure", arXiv:2310.10351, 2023** — feature-based local tolerance per intersection edge t = k · d(f1) · d(f2) · d(e0) · t0 with d = maximum polynomial degree and k = max(1, fitting errors / t0) (see Q4 entry). — [Huang 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.10351)

Solid Modeling 1993. Jackson characterises the 1993 CAD paper as an "adaptive tolerance-based approach [that] associate[s] tolerances with individual points, curves and surfaces, and detect[s] ambiguous situations. When an ambiguity is detected, the tolerances have to be increased, and some or all of the algorithm re-run", on a hybrid CSG/B-rep. Fang's 1992 Utah dissertation abstract (https://collections.lib.utah.edu/details?id=108508) describes "an analytic model method for low-degree curves and surfaces and an approximated model method for high-degree ones". ### Inferences
- Both Jackson and Segal keep *geometry exact and topology tolerant*: tolerance is attached to vertices/edges/faces and grown only by merging. cadrs already has per-entity tolerances; the missing pieces are (1) a formal validity test (regions of non-adjacent entities disjoint; adjacent overlaps connected), (2) local re-intersection after a merge (Segal's near-value trigger, OCCT's sub-PaveFiller) instead of a global rerun, and (3) the ordering rule enforced after every operation (a slab/tube/sphere ordering).
- Tolerance growth: nobody has a general bound. The practical mitigations in the sources are (a) Segal's "report large tolerances", (b) OCCT's non-destructive mode (never raise input tolerances; only the result carries them), (c) Jackson's advice to keep geometry exact so tolerance only measures topology mismatch, and (d) Segal's suggested post-process relaxation to shrink tolerances (unimplemented). cadrs' `fit_tolerances` after a boolean is the (d) that exists; the retry ladder 1e-6/1e-5/1e-4 is a bounded growth by design.
- The Virtual-Interval semantics implies cadrs' retry condition "open shell" is the right *detector* (an open shell means S_in / S_out are not separated by the thickened boundary) but the right *response* is local: raise the tolerance of only the entities around the gap (Jackson's re-sew with larger edge tolerances).

### Gaps
- Fang & Brüderlin's actual algorithms (how tolerances are increased and which steps rerun) were not readable — only citations.
- Shen, Patrikalakis & Shapiro on validity under tolerance: no paper with that author triple was found; the closest found is the Qi–Shapiro–Stewart work above and Patrikalakis' Shape Interrogation hyperbook (interval arithmetic chapters). The "Shen" reference may be G. Shen's work with Patrikalakis on interval B-reps ("boundary representation model rectification", 2001), not located.
- The Parasolid documentation of tolerant modelling (public "Parasolid Functional Description") was not reachable.

## Q3. Robust arithmetic: which techniques apply to curved B-reps rather than polyhedra?

### Takeaway
For polyhedra the field has a complete answer (plane-based representation + exact or filtered integer predicates: Sugihara–Iri, Fortune, Shewchuk, Nehring-Wirxel), and no digit proliferation occurs because the output inherits the input planes. For curved B-reps exact computation is only practical for low-degree algebraic surfaces (ESOLID: ≤ degree 4, 1–2 orders of magnitude slower, Yu's bound of L^720 bits for separating quadric intersections); what transfers is the *structure* of those systems — floating-point filters with error bounds, lazy exact evaluation only on filter failure, interval arithmetic for quick rejection and for certifying root isolation, and "ask every incidence question through one canonical predicate on the original geometry" — plus Shewchuk's exact predicates for the planar/linear subset (plane–plane–plane vertices, point-vs-plane, polygonal loops in uv).

### Cited Findings

**[Rank 11] Shewchuk, J. R., "Adaptive precision floating-point arithmetic and fast robust geometric predicates", Discrete & Computational Geometry 18:305–363, 1997** (conference: "Robust adaptive floating-point geometric predicates", SoCG 1996, pp. 141–150; CMU TR CMU-CS-96-140). DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/PL00009321. Open PDFs: journal version https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~jrs/papers/robustr.pdf, conference version https://www.wias-berlin.de/people/si/course/files/Shewchuk96-predicates.pdf; public-domain C code https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~quake/robust.html. Local: `.../Shewchuk-1997-adaptive-precision-DCG.pdf`, `.../Shewchuk-1996-robust-predicates.pdf`.
Method: exact arithmetic on floating-point *expansions* (sums of non-overlapping doubles) using Dekker/Knuth error-free transformations (two-sum, two-product), and adaptive orientation/incircle predicates evaluated in stages (fast floating-point with a static error bound, then successively more exact terms) so the cost "depend[s] on the degree of uncertainty of the result, and is usually small". Covers 2D/3D orientation and incircle/insphere. — [Shewchuk abstract](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~jrs/papers/robust-predicates.abstract)
Limitations: predicates are polynomials in input coordinates; no square roots or transcendental functions; useful only when the inputs are the ground-truth coordinates (not intermediate constructions, unless composed as in Attene's indirect predicates).
Relevance to cadrs: `split` and `classify` for the planar parts: exact orient2d in uv for loop orientation, point-in-loop and the CDT used by `tess`; exact orient3d for point-vs-plane, coplanarity of planar faces and line–plane crossing; a Rust port of the expansion arithmetic is small (the `robust` crate implements these predicates).

**[Rank 8] Hoffmann, C. M., "Robustness in geometric computations", ASME J. Computing and Information Science in Engineering 1(2):143–155, 2001.** DOI https://doi.org/10.1115/1.1375815. Author PDF https://www.cs.purdue.edu/cgvlab/www/resources/papers/Hoffmann-JCISE-2001-Robustness_in_Geometric_Computations.pdf. Local: `.../Hoffmann-2001-robustness-in-geometric-computations.pdf`. Also Hoffmann, "The problems of accuracy and robustness in geometric computation", IEEE Computer 22(3):31–39, 1989 (DOI https://doi.org/10.1109/2.16223, local `.../Hoffmann-1989-problems-accuracy-robustness.pdf`) and Hoffmann, Hopcroft, Karasick, "Towards implementing robust geometric computations", SoCG 1988 (local `.../Hoffmann-1988-towards-robust-geometric-computations.pdf`).
Problem statement: "the same logical question may arise repeatedly, in the form of different floating-point computations giving contradictory answers" (an edge crossing a cube's top face steeply and its front face at a shallow angle gets the top-face hit classified interior and the front-face hit on an edge, so the edge "intersects the top face twice"). Three strategies: exact arithmetic, symbolic reasoning, reliable (interval) calculation. — [Hoffmann 2001](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/cgvlab/www/resources/papers/Hoffmann-JCISE-2001-Robustness_in_Geometric_Computations.pdf)
Exact arithmetic for polyhedra (Sugihara & Iri 1989/1992): represent only face-plane coefficients (B-bit integers, d with 2B bits), vertices as determinant ratios, incidence as a 4×4 determinant = 0 needing "quintuple precision"; "the output polyhedron inherits all surfaces from the two input polyhedra ... no digit proliferation takes place"; rotations break representability, handled by re-evaluating the CSG tree from rotated trihedral primitives. Fortune 1997 ("Polyhedral modelling with multiprecision integer arithmetic", CAD 29(2):123–133): same plane representation, tolerates self-intersection after rigid motion and extracts a legal "core"; a preprocessor (Fortune & Van Wyk) decides per polynomial predicate whether floating point suffices and emits C++; "running time increased by about a third due to the times that exact arithmetic was used, in less than 10% of the cases". Curved primitives: for quadrics, Yu's thesis derives "an estimate of L^720 for the precision required to separate two intersections of quadric surfaces. This clearly eliminates a straightforward, exact-arithmetic strategy for curved geometric primitives". — [Hoffmann 2001](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/cgvlab/www/resources/papers/Hoffmann-JCISE-2001-Robustness_in_Geometric_Computations.pdf)
Symbolic reasoning for polyhedral intersection: reduce all incidence decisions to two *primary* ε-judgements — v on face plane f, and e ∩ e′ (intersect e with one face plane adjacent to e′ and check the point is on all other planes adjacent to e′) — and derive the rest: v on e (v on every face plane adjacent to e), v = v′ (v on every plane incident to v′ and vice versa), e on f, e collinear e′, e ∩ f (vertices on opposite sides, or one on f), f ∩ f′ (every vertex of each on the other's plane); "there is a need to iterate incidence tests because, as vertices are placed into faces and face planes, the incidence decision of the higher-dimensional entities are affected"; consistent decisions require "posting an incidence choice to all affected other intersections" and being able to *undo* an earlier non-incidence decision; nearness is not transitive (A≈B, B≈C but A≉C), and a vertex can be on both adjacent planes yet not on their common edge (Fig. 9). Advice: compute the three-plane intersection with QR rather than determinants. — [Hoffmann 2001](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/cgvlab/www/resources/papers/Hoffmann-JCISE-2001-Robustness_in_Geometric_Computations.pdf)
Interval computation: floating-point intervals [x̲, x̄] with outward rounding so that "the machine arithmetic used does not violate the enclosure property"; interval extensions of functions; the key objective "an interval enclosure that is as tight as possible". — [Hoffmann 2001](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/cgvlab/www/resources/papers/Hoffmann-JCISE-2001-Robustness_in_Geometric_Computations.pdf)
Relevance to cadrs: `boolean` (the primary/secondary predicate hierarchy is a design rule: every V-V, V-E, E-E, V-F, E-F, F-F coincidence test in cadrs should be *derived* from one or two primitive tests on the exact geometry so the same question cannot get two answers — e.g. "vertex on surface within tol" and "edge curve meets surface within tol" as primitives, with vertex=vertex meaning each lies on all surfaces of the other); `tol` (expect non-transitivity and plan the pool merge as a connexity-chain union, as OCCT does).

**[Rank 10] Nehring-Wirxel, J., Trettner, P., Kobbelt, L., "Fast exact Booleans for iterated CSG using octree-embedded BSPs", Computer-Aided Design 135:103015, 2021.** DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cad.2021.103015; arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.02486, PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.02486. Local: `.../NehringWirxel-2021-octree-bsp-booleans.pdf`.
Method: geometry is a BSP of planes with integer coefficients; vertices are never stored as coordinates but as 4D homogeneous integer intersections of three planes (four 3×3 determinants, Cramer), so classifying a vertex against a plane is a 4D dot product times sign(x4) — "the costly 4×4 determinant ... is split into the construction of the intersection points (precompute four 3×3 determinants) and the actual classification (a 4D dot product)". All arithmetic is fixed-width 128/192/256-bit integer with an a-priori overflow analysis: with b-bit operations, exact and overflow-free iff n+^4 · v+ ≤ 2^(b−1)/48 (Hadamard bound), i.e. for 256 bits "all integer meshes with positions smaller than 8.73·10^7, or slightly above 26 bit" when normals come from cross products; "For a simulation with a bounding volume of 1 m³, we get a resolution of 0.22 mm for 128 bit, 1.58 µm for 192 bit, and 11.5 nm for 256 bit". Because "CSG operations cannot introduce new planes, all possible positions are representable using combinations of input planes" (no proliferation). Core operation is cutting a convex half-edge mesh by a plane with *edge descent* (walk to the vertex nearest the plane; if no vertex is on the other side the plane misses) then *marching* around the cut faces, so only vertices near the cut are classified; booleans are Naylor-style BSP merges reformulated with this cutting; a redundancy-removal step extracts the boundary and rebuilds the BSP after every merge; BSPs of ≤ 100–200 nodes are embedded in a persistent global octree whose cells split when a BSP grows, with a flood-fill to label empty cells; about 2.5 M mesh-plane cuts/s single core. — [Nehring-Wirxel 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.02486)
Limitations: planar faces only; convex cells; input must be quantised to integers once; re-triangulation of cut faces is impossible without new planes, so faces stay convex polygons.
Relevance to cadrs: `classify`/`split` only for the all-planar subset (sheet-metal-like bodies, cells of planar arrangements): a plane-based exact representation of planar faces with homogeneous-integer vertices would make cadrs' planar-only booleans and `cells` exact. The transferable general idea is the *overflow analysis* that chooses a precision given the model's `SIZE` box (cadrs `tol::SIZE` 1e8 mm), and the predicate caching trick (store per plane the four cofactors) which Cherchi 2022 also uses.

**[Rank 16] Foskey, M., Manocha, D., Culver, T., Keyser, J., Krishnan, S., "Reliable geometric computations with algebraic primitives and predicates", Workshop on Uncertainty in Geometric Computations, Kluwer 2001/2002.** PDF http://gamma.cs.unc.edu/ESOLID/foskey02.pdf. Local: `.../Foskey-2001-reliable-algebraic-primitives-predicates.pdf`.
Content: "the distinction between exact arithmetic and exact computation — the only explicit representation of a point is inexact [an isolating rational box], but all comparisons are made exactly"; points defined by two algebraic plane curves are isolated with univariate Sturm sequences on box boundaries; floating-point filters used wherever a sign test is needed; Aberth–Schaefer *range arithmetic* (arbitrary-length floating point with an interval radius, recomputed at higher precision when insufficient) fills "the large gap between the 53 mantissa bits ... and the hundreds of bits that can arise in a Sturm sequence". — [Foskey 2001](http://gamma.cs.unc.edu/ESOLID/foskey02.pdf)
Relevance to cadrs: `ssi` / `boolean`: the "isolating box + refine on demand" representation of an intersection *point* (edge–face hit, vertex of three faces) is the curved-surface analogue of Shewchuk's exact point; cadrs could carry an error radius with each computed intersection point and refine (Newton on the two/three surfaces) only when a tolerance decision is within that radius.

**ESOLID speedup catalogue** (Keyser 2002 §5, same entry as Q1): lazy evaluation of point intervals and curve subdivision; quick rejection by interval/affine arithmetic and bounding boxes; simplified computation (qualitative tags for near-instant equality, exploiting horizontal/vertical curves in the limited domain, polynomial sign tests instead of full Sturm when the root is known simple); lower-dimensional formulation (all computations in 2D domains); floating-point filters and "floating-point guided computation" (guess roots by Newton, verify by Sturm counts); arbitrary-precision floating point (PRECISE). Layered: filter → range arithmetic → exact. Note "some speedup techniques tend to conflict (lazy evaluation wants large intervals, floating-point guided computation wants tight ones)". — [Keyser 2002](http://gamma.cs.unc.edu/ESOLID/keyser02.pdf)

**Indirect predicates and lazy exact kernels (via Cherchi 2022 and Zhou 2016):** Attene, "Indirect predicates for geometric constructions", Computer-Aided Design 126:102856, 2020 — an intersection point is kept implicitly as (line, plane) or (plane, plane, plane) and "the point's expression can be composed with the predicate's expression, enabling the use of arithmetic filtering"; Zhou 2016 uses CGAL's `Lazy_exact_nt` (filtered, exact rationals on demand) and notes conversion of exact output to floating point is "a potential source of problems ... observed in a very small proportion of cases"; Cherchi 2022: "arithmetic filtering can only be employed if the predicate input is guaranteed correct ... for the case of mesh Booleans the only intermediate constructions are the intersection points". — [Cherchi 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.14151); [Zhou 2016](http://www.cs.columbia.edu/cg/mesh-arrangements/mesh-arrangements-for-solid-geometry-siggraph-2016-zhou-et-al.pdf)

**Interval arithmetic for curved SSI (Patrikalakis, Maekawa, Cho, "Shape Interrogation for Computer Aided Design and Manufacturing", Springer 2002/2009, free hyperbook http://web.mit.edu/hyperbook/Patrikalakis-Maekawa-Cho/):** Chapter 4 covers rounded interval arithmetic and the Interval Projected Polyhedron (IPP) solver for polynomial systems; Chapter 5 recasts surface–surface intersection as such systems; Chapter 6 gives intersection-curve derivatives for robust tracing (from the hyperbook table of contents; not downloaded as PDF). — [Hyperbook](http://web.mit.edu/hyperbook/Patrikalakis-Maekawa-Cho/node1.html)

**Snap rounding:** Cherchi 2022 §2.3 summarises the state: converting exact/implicit coordinates to floats "may invalidate the model by introducing degenerate or intersecting elements. A provably correct and efficient solution to this problem is still elusive and existing algorithms are either impractical [Devillers et al. 2018] or do not guarantee to produce a correct result in all the cases [Milenkovic and Sacks 2019]"; Diazzi & Attene 2021 likewise: "no algorithm is still known to resolve [3D snap rounding] in an acceptable amount of time". — [Cherchi 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.14151); [Diazzi 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.14434)

### Inferences
- For cadrs (B-splines, tori, surfaces of revolution) exact computation is out; the applicable pattern is ESOLID/Cherchi's *cascade*: cheap float test with an error bound → tighter test (interval / refined Newton with residual) → an expensive certified test only when the decision is within the bound. cadrs' named round-off margins (`tol::REL`, `ROUNDOFF`) are the static error bounds of the first stage; what is missing is the second stage.
- Exact predicates are still worth adopting for the planar/linear subset: planar faces, line edges, uv polygon loops, the CDT in `tess`, and plane–plane–plane vertices — where Shewchuk/Nehring-Wirxel give exactness for free and where most "touching box" test failures occur.
- The design rule that matters most for curved geometry is Hoffmann's: a small set of primary predicates on the *original* geometry, everything else derived, with the ability to undo a non-incidence decision when a later merge raises a tolerance (Segal's near value).

### Gaps
- Fortune 1997 and Sugihara–Iri are known only through Hoffmann 2001; Yu's thesis (quadric precision bound) not located.
- No open-access paper was found that applies filtered/interval predicates to NURBS–NURBS intersection inside a boolean; Patrikalakis' hyperbook covers the solver but not the boolean.

## Q4. Coincidence handling: overlapping and tangent faces, edges on faces, shared faces, imprints

### Takeaway
All robust systems convert coincidence into shared topology *before* classification and classify "on" pieces as a third state carrying orientation; they differ in how they find coincidence. Tolerant systems (Jackson, OCCT) find it bottom-up (V-V, V-E, E-E, V-F, E-F, then F-F) and skip the unstable surface–surface intersection wherever lower-dimensional comparisons already established the contact. Exact mesh systems (Zhou) keep duplicate triangles and zero-volume cells and let the extraction function remove them; topology-oriented systems (Smith/Manifold) remove coincidence by symbolic perturbation and clean up afterwards. Huang 2023 is the one B-rep paper that repairs tangent-face artefacts (short edges that should be vertices, disconnected intersection edges) by set-theoretic inference on the intersection graph.

### Cited Findings

**Jackson 1995 (entry in Q2):** coincidence order and the face-face skip rule quoted above; a vertex "can correspond to an edge, face or even a 3-space region (cell), if the tolerances so indicate"; sewing is the boolean without E-F and F-F. — [Jackson 1995](https://ftp1.cs.wisc.edu/pub/users/prem/jackson-SM-95.pdf)

**OCCT (entry in Q1):** E-E and E-F EDGE common parts become common blocks; SD faces found by `AreFacesSameDomain` and chained; Gluing modes: `BOPAlgo_GlueShift` (partial coincidence, skips FACE/FACE intersection) and `BOPAlgo_GlueFull` (full coincidence, skips VERTEX/FACE, EDGE/FACE and FACE/FACE), "up to 90%" speedup, invalid when there are real intersections and unchecked. History rule: "CUT of fully coincident faces deletes both faces. Partial overlap keeps the object face." — [OCCT spec](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/overview/html/specification__boolean_operations.html)

**Segal 1990 (entry in Q2):** coplanar faces are never partitioned against each other; the third face sharing an edge induces the partition; a face whose edges all lie in the other's plane is deemed coplanar even if the intersection line's tolerance is small; an edge whose endpoints lie off the other plane but whose tube is contained in it raises the plane's tolerance (and may backtrack). — [Segal 1990](https://ftp1.cs.wisc.edu/pub/users/prem/segal-repair-siggraph-1990.pdf)

**HHK 1989 (entry in Q1):** coincident edges handled by merging the radially ordered directed-edge lists of the two edges and transferring to faces between volume-enclosing pairs; areas on both surfaces classified oninB/onoutB "can be ignored [when adding B's faces], as they were classified as oninB and have already been accounted for". — [HHK 1989](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/cstech/624)

**Zhou 2016 (entry in Q5):** "co-planar and duplicate facets ... do not necessarily invalidate a PWN mesh"; co-planar clusters get one shared CDT so overlapping triangles are subdivided consistently ("Independent triangulation could lead to inconsistency", Fig. 10); "Duplicate triangles need to be retained at this stage, as their removal requires knowledge of the extraction function"; zero-volume cells "always bound by exactly two clones of the same geometric triangle" get winding numbers like any cell; duplicates are ordered around an edge by signed index (simulation of simplicity) and purged at extraction by zero total signed occurrence. — [Zhou 2016](http://www.cs.columbia.edu/cg/mesh-arrangements/mesh-arrangements-for-solid-geometry-siggraph-2016-zhou-et-al.pdf)

**[Rank 9] Smith 2009 / Manifold library.** Smith's basic boolean is "topology-oriented, as defined in [Sugihara, Iri, Inagaki, Imai 2000]": a double pyramid of 16 relation operations by entity-dimension sum (levels 0–6), where level-3 results (vertex-in-solid, edge∩facet with the point) drive construction and "symbolic perturbation rules are applied ... the vertex will never be considered to lie on the surface (even when it does) ... [edge and facet] either intersect fully or not at all"; result edges are built from *net end-vertex counts* (±1 per inclusion), paired by sorting along the composite edge; it guarantees topologically valid output "irrespective of the extent of numerical errors", with winding-number consistency u = a + b − ab, i = ab, d = a − ab, but "geometric validity of the result is not assured" and a *data-smoothing* post-process (merge near-coincident vertices, remove zero-length edges, cancel opposing coincident half-edges and facets, "edge-cracking" to split an edge near a vertex or edge, partial facet cancellation) is applied; the smoothing "is not fully robust ... operations often make facets non-planar". For two touching cubes the perturbation direction decides whether the shared facet is a zero-area face or a zero-thickness gap. On curved domains Smith concludes "it is hard to see how a topologically robust Boolean algorithm could be devised" for ACIS-like representations, singling out "data drift, with an edge or vertex not positioned precisely on a face it is presumed to border". — [Smith 2009](https://github.com/elalish/manifold/blob/master/docs/RobustBoolean.pdf)
Manifold (Lalish, https://github.com/elalish/manifold/wiki/Manifold-Library): "Rather than relying on exact arithmetic, the method accepts floating-point error and aims for a result that is 'manifold by construction'. Each geometric computation builds on earlier ones, so the same question is never answered two different ways"; "the Boolean uses no tolerance. Ties between geometry that is exactly equal on any axis are broken by perturbing one mesh. For a Union, the first mesh is perturbed along its surface normal; for a Difference or Intersection, the opposite direction is used. Touching cubes merge, equal-height differences yield through-holes, and a mesh minus itself is empty"; broad phase is a parallel BVH (Karras 2012) and "the broad phase can take longer in practice than the intersection work itself"; result polygons are ear-clipped and degenerate triangles collapsed; "degenerate triangles are not guaranteed to be fully removed, since doing so can require perturbing geometry by more than ε". — [Manifold wiki](https://github.com/elalish/manifold/wiki/Manifold-Library)

**[Rank 13] Huang, H., Liang, S., et al., "B-rep Boolean resulting model repair by correcting intersection edges based on inference procedure", arXiv:2310.10351, 2023.** PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.10351. Local: `.../Huang-2023-brep-boolean-repair.pdf`.
Method: the boolean is intersection → imprint → classification → merge; "the main reason why boolean operations fail is the intersection inconsistency caused by errors", classified as face-face, edge-face, edge-edge. Before imprinting, intersection edges are organised in an *intersection graph* (edges connected at same-position vertices, each with predecessor/successor, branching allowed) and each edge gets a feature-based tolerance t = k·d(f1)·d(f2)·d(e0)·t0. Inference rules on topological sets (e ∈ f ⇒ e ∩ f = e; v ∈ e ∩ f ⇒ v = e ∩ f locally) prove, e.g., that two intersection edges e01 = f0∩f1 and e02 = f0∩f2 whose nearby endpoints both lie on the common edge e12 of f1, f2 must meet at f0 ∩ e12, so both vertices are snapped to the *edge–face* intersection (lower dimension, "lower errors"); a "pretty short" intersection edge (shorter than its tolerance, connected at ≤ 1 end, whose end vertices are within tolerance of the intersection v0 of the two faces' boundary edges) is the remnant of two faces touching at a vertex or tangentially and is collapsed to v0. Tested on "hundreds of thousands of tests on the common shapes of industrial parts". — [Huang 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.10351)
Limitations: heuristic tolerance formula; repairs only the enumerated patterns; arXiv preprint without a listed venue.
Relevance to cadrs: `boolean` (an intersection graph with per-edge tolerance between F-F intersection and imprint is a cheap place to apply the two repairs above — snapping F-F curve ends onto E-F hits of the shared boundary edge is the same principle as Jackson's "skip F-F where E-F already found the contact" and Krishnan's "merge curves only if the generating surfaces are adjacent"); `tol` (degree-scaled per-edge tolerance as an alternative to a global retry ladder).

**Krishnan 1997 §6.3 and §7.2:** degeneracies specific to curved solids include face-face and edge-edge overlaps "handled as special cases in our system"; the near-boundary classification uses the sign of the trimming curve's polynomial (SVD). — [Krishnan 1997](https://www.cs.unc.edu/xcms/wpfiles/dissertations/krishnan.pdf)

**Cherchi 2022 (entry in Q5):** "an output triangle can belong to many input meshes, for example in the case of meshes that overlap at a coplanar region"; rays that hit a vertex or edge are perturbed with `std::nextafter` on the far point until the crossing is interior; "the result of a Boolean operation between two watertight manifold meshes that do not touch tangentially is guaranteed to be manifold watertight". — [Cherchi 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.14151)

### Inferences
- cadrs' "coincident faces imprinted and coincident pieces made one edge" is the Jackson/OCCT common-block approach. The two refinements the literature agrees on are: (1) detect contact bottom-up and *suppress the F-F surface intersection* for face pairs whose contact is already represented by common edges (Jackson; OCCT GlueShift does it globally; Huang does it by snapping), because near-tangent SSI is where the pipeline becomes unstable; (2) classify "on" pieces with the two faces' relative sense (HHK oninB/onoutB; OCCT's SD faces have one representative whose orientation is chosen per operation), so unions keep one copy, intersections keep one copy, and subtractions drop both.
- The mesh systems' "keep duplicates until extraction" (Zhou) is equivalent to cadrs keeping both imprinted faces until the operation decides; it argues for *not* merging coincident faces during imprint but tagging them as a pair and deciding at classification.
- Manifold's op-dependent perturbation direction is a neat rule for exactly-touching inputs (touching boxes fuse, equal-height cut goes through) that cadrs can implement at the tolerance level: for union treat gaps < tol as contact, for subtract treat overlaps < tol as through.

### Gaps
- No paper found that describes an *imprint* operation (as a standalone kernel API like ACIS `api_imprint`) in detail; Jackson's imprint phase and OCCT's Splitter/Section are the closest open descriptions.
- Tangent-face (G1-contact) boolean handling for curved surfaces specifically (e.g. a fillet surface tangent to a face) is not covered by any found paper beyond Jackson's remark and Keyser's example of BOOLE failing on near-tangent cylinders.

## Q5. 2015–2026 mesh-arrangement booleans: which classification/propagation ideas transfer to curved B-reps?

### Takeaway
Three ideas transfer directly: (1) Zhou's cell–patch graph with winding-number *vectors* propagated combinatorially from one seeded cell (variadic booleans and "at least k of n" for free, one classification per connected component, nested components joined by one point location each); (2) Cherchi's exact single ray per patch with a cascade from cheap-approximate to exact, axis-aligned to reduce ray–face tests to 2D, perturbing the far point on vertex/edge hits; (3) Manifold's and Smith's symbolic-perturbation convention for exact contact. Generalized winding numbers (Jacobson 2013, Barill 2018) transfer only as a fallback classifier for broken inputs. Plane-based integer BSPs (Nehring-Wirxel, EMBER) do not transfer to curved faces.

### Cited Findings

**[Rank 3] Zhou, Q., Grinspun, E., Zorin, D., Jacobson, A., "Mesh arrangements for solid geometry", ACM Transactions on Graphics 35(4), Article 39 (SIGGRAPH 2016).** DOI https://doi.org/10.1145/2897824.2925901. Project page http://www.cs.columbia.edu/cg/mesh-arrangements/; paper PDF http://www.cs.columbia.edu/cg/mesh-arrangements/mesh-arrangements-for-solid-geometry-siggraph-2016-zhou-et-al.pdf; supplemental ...-supplemental-zhou-et-al.pdf; slides .../mesh-arrangements-siggraph.pdf. Local: `.../Zhou-2016-mesh-arrangements.pdf`, `.../Zhou-2016-mesh-arrangements-supplemental.pdf`, `.../Zhou-2016-mesh-arrangements-slides.pdf`. Code in libigl.
Algorithm: inputs are n piecewise-constant-winding-number (PWN) meshes with rational coordinates; an extraction function f(w) on the winding-number vector w = [w1..wn] selects cells (union: ∃ wi ≠ 0; intersection: ∀ wi ≠ 0; A\B: w1 ≠ 0 ∧ w2 = 0; "at least two": ∃ i≠j wi, wj ≠ 0; complements by wj = 1 − |wi| or 1 − wi). Stage 1 *intersection resolution*: drop zero-area triangles, intersect all triangle pairs exactly (CGAL, bounding-box culling, parallel), cluster coplanar intersecting triangles and compute one CDT per cluster (constraints subdivided at intersections so no Steiner points are needed), replicate sub-triangles into each original triangle with a back-reference, purge duplicate vertices by lexicographic sort, *retain duplicate triangles*. Stage 2 *cells*: patches = maximal sets of triangles connected across manifold edges; for each connected component build a bipartite cell–patch graph by sorting the patches around one representative non-manifold edge per incident patch pair and unioning the "above"/"below" cell references consecutively; zero-volume cells arise from duplicate triangles; nested components are joined by one point location per component (closest point on the mesh, then a dummy facet inserted into the cyclic order around an edge at that point — "only at edges can we robustly determine the symbolic and geometric cell arrangement"); the ambient cell of a component is found from the vertex of maximal |x| and the incident edge of maximum |ey/ex| (Attene 2014's normal-based choice fails at non-manifold vertices, Fig. 12). Stage 3 *labelling*: seed the universal ambient cell with w = 0 and BFS, wn ← wc + sp·δi across each patch p of mesh i (sp = ±1 by side). Stage 4 *extraction*: flag cells with f true, output the patches between flagged and unflagged cells with orientation fixed, purge zero-signed-occurrence duplicate triangles. Sorting triangles about an edge is a recursive four-group split with simulation of simplicity for coplanar/duplicate triangles, indices signed by edge incidence for consistency across edges (Fig. 13). Output is an exact "solid mesh" (no self-intersections, no degenerate or duplicate triangles, winding 0/1); float conversion is a post-process with a heuristic for the rare failures. Tested on 10,000 Thingiverse meshes, of which only 54.7 % were free of self-intersections. — [Zhou 2016](http://www.cs.columbia.edu/cg/mesh-arrangements/mesh-arrangements-for-solid-geometry-siggraph-2016-zhou-et-al.pdf)
Limitations: triangles only; exact rationals (slow: libigl runs "roughly 1–2 fps for just 50K triangles" per Cherchi 2022); open boundaries only if they meet along seams; float output can self-intersect in rare cases.
Relevance to cadrs: `classify` (replace per-piece ray parity with: one point classification per connected arrangement component to seed, then propagate a *vector* of per-body states across every intersection edge; the same arrangement then serves union/intersect/subtract/`cells` without re-classifying — cadrs' `Kernel::cells` is Zhou's arrangement with a material per cell, like OCCT's Cells Builder); `topo` (the cyclic sort of faces about an edge, with a deterministic tie rule for coincident faces, is the one primitive both Zhou and HHK rest on; cadrs needs it for non-manifold intermediate results and shell assembly); `boolean_kinds` (variadic extraction functions subsume "fuse of many tools" and "at least k").

**[Rank 6] Cherchi, G., Pellacini, F., Attene, M., Livesu, M., "Interactive and robust mesh Booleans", ACM Transactions on Graphics 41(6):1–14, Article 248 (SIGGRAPH Asia 2022).** DOI https://doi.org/10.1145/3550454.3555460. arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14151, PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.14151; publisher OA copy via https://iris.unimore.it/handle/11380/1299687. Local: `.../Cherchi-2022-interactive-robust-mesh-booleans.pdf`. Builds on Cherchi, Livesu, Scateni, Attene, "Fast and robust mesh arrangements using floating-point arithmetic", ACM TOG 39(6), 2020 (OA copy at https://iris.unica.it/retrieve/e4fd9a6e-f57a-4379-8266-f97fc9aaaf02/mesh_arrangement.pdf — download blocked by a Cloudflare challenge, not saved).
Algorithm: Step 1 arrangement (from Cherchi 2020): detect triangle intersections with an octree, insert intersection points then segments; intersection points are *implicit* (Attene 2020 LPI/TPI) so all predicates stay filtered; orient3d is split into four cached 3×3 cofactors per input plane so each call is "a simple scalar product in 4D"; segment insertion uses Livesu 2021's O(n) polygon triangulation; parallel data structures (swiss tables, arenas, small-vector adjacency). Step 2 classification: for each *patch* (bounded by intersection loops), cast one ray from an interior point to a far point p∞ along a major axis beyond the scene box, compute and sort intersections with each input mesh exactly (LPI points, exact comparator), take the first hit and decide in/out by the sign of the tetrahedron (hit triangle, p∞) — "the algorithm scales with the number of patches in the arrangement and not with the number of triangles". Ray origin cascade: try a random triangle of the patch, snap its implicit vertices to floats, use its barycentre pushed back along the axis and verify by a 2D point-in-triangle test that the axis-aligned ray still crosses the triangle; try up to a few more triangles; "if no valid triangle can be found, we compute the exact barycenter with rational numbers and perform an exact ray casting" — in 3.8K booleans / 80K rays, 2 % needed snap rounding, 97 % succeeded at the first triangle, the exact fallback was never hit. Vertex/edge hits are resolved by perturbing p∞ with `nextafter` (then the ray is no longer axis aligned and a 3-orient3d test is used); never triggered on real shapes, only on a mesh against its own translate. — [Cherchi 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.14151)
Results: interactive to ~100–120K triangles on an M1 Pro; on 3.3–21.6 M-triangle pairs "almost 25× faster than libigl" (Zhou 2016) overall and >5× faster on the arrangement alone; classification up to 100× faster than topological flooding (Attene 2014) or patch-graph propagation (Zhou 2016) and scales better on variadic booleans of hundreds of inputs; "Booleans become increasingly complex when the two shapes are almost perfectly aligned". — [Cherchi 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.14151)
Limitations: inputs must be manifold, watertight, non-self-intersecting; triangles; the exact arrangement is the precondition for the ray approach ("non-robust techniques cannot guarantee the absence of gaps or tiny topological channels").
Relevance to cadrs: `classify` — this is the modern form of cadrs' ray parity: one ray per connected piece (not per face), axis-aligned so curve/surface hits reduce to a lower-dimensional test, a *cascade* (float ray against a point well inside the piece → if the hit is within tolerance of an edge/vertex or the start point is within tolerance of the boundary, perturb the far point and retry → only then an expensive exact/interval ray), and the explicit requirement that the arrangement be closed before classifying (which is what cadrs' open-shell retry checks, but after the fact).

**[Rank 15] Barill, G., Dickson, N., Schmidt, R., Levin, D. I. W., Jacobson, A., "Fast winding numbers for soups and clouds", ACM TOG 37(4), Article 43 (SIGGRAPH 2018).** DOI https://doi.org/10.1145/3197517.3201337; project https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/projects/fast-winding-numbers/; PDF https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/projects/fast-winding-numbers/fast-winding-numbers-for-soups-and-clouds-siggraph-2018-barill-et-al.pdf. Local: `.../Barill-2018-fast-winding-numbers.pdf`. And **Jacobson, Kavan, Sorkine-Hornung, "Robust inside-outside segmentation using generalized winding numbers", ACM TOG 32(4) (SIGGRAPH 2013)**, PDF https://igl.ethz.ch/projects/winding-number/robust-inside-outside-segmentation-using-generalized-winding-numbers-siggraph-2013-jacobson-et-al.pdf, local `.../Jacobson-2013-generalized-winding-numbers.pdf`.
Content: the generalized winding number (sum of signed solid angles / 4π) gives a smooth, defect-tolerant inside/outside field for soups with holes and self-intersections; Barill accelerates it with a tree and far-field (dipole) expansion so it is usable for "defect-tolerant mesh booleans" (each mesh's elements are classified by the other's approximate winding field). Cherchi 2022 characterises it as "efficient at query time but approximate and require initialization". — [Barill project](https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/projects/fast-winding-numbers/); [Cherchi 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.14151)
Relevance to cadrs: `classify` fallback for `meshsolid`/healed bodies whose shells are not closed: a solid-angle integral over the tessellated faces (or exact over planar faces) classifies a piece robustly when parity fails; also the natural definition of "inside" for Zhou-style winding vectors.

**[Rank 17] Diazzi, L., Attene, M., "Convex polyhedral meshing for robust solid modeling", ACM TOG 40(6), Article 259 (SIGGRAPH Asia 2021).** DOI https://doi.org/10.1145/3478513.3480564; arXiv PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.14434. Local: `.../Diazzi-2021-convex-polyhedral-meshing.pdf`. A Delaunay tetrahedrisation of the input vertices is split by the input planes (TetWild-like) with indirect predicates so every vertex is a direct expression of input coordinates, giving a convex-polyhedral volume mesh exactly conformal to the input; booleans and Minkowski sums then become cell selection and are "surprisingly robust" even on defective input (10,000 Thingi10k models). — [Diazzi 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.14434). Relevance: `cells`/`meshsolid` for polyhedral inputs only.

**Nehring-Wirxel 2021** (entry in Q3). Related, not downloaded: Trettner, Nehring-Wirxel, Kobbelt, "EMBER: Exact mesh Booleans via efficient & robust local arrangements", ACM TOG 41(4), SIGGRAPH 2022 (the RWTH publication server refused connections); and the "winding number vector" octree method cited by Liang/Huang 2025 as Trettner et al. [13].

### Inferences
- cadrs' "ray parity with in/out propagation through connected groups" is already the Zhou/Krishnan scheme in spirit; the concrete upgrades are (a) a per-body state *vector* per piece so one arrangement serves all operations and `cells`, (b) Cherchi's cascade for the one ray each component needs, with the ray chosen axis-aligned from a point of a face interior at distance > tolerance from the piece boundary, and (c) deterministic cyclic ordering of faces about an edge for assembly.
- Mesh methods get their robustness from an *exact arrangement*; cadrs cannot have that for curved faces, so its arrangement must instead be made *consistent by construction* (Jackson/HHK: every coincidence decided once, bottom-up, and posted to all faces) — the classification stage can then be as simple as Cherchi's.

### Gaps
- EMBER 2022 and Cherchi 2020 PDFs were not obtained (server refused / Cloudflare), so their local-arrangement and floating-point-arrangement details are known only via Cherchi 2022 and Nehring-Wirxel 2021.

## Q6. Performance: what dominates and what indexing/parallelism is used?

### Takeaway
For curved solids the intersection stage dominates (ESOLID: 54–98 % in curve–curve intersection; OCCT's 45× fuzzy and up-to-90 % gluing speedups are both about avoiding face-face intersection), so the published wins are in culling face pairs (AABB sweep / BVH / OBB, then a separating-plane LP on control polytopes, then face clustering) and in skipping SSI where contact is already known. For meshes the broad phase and the exact intersection are the costs and are parallelised; classification is made negligible by doing it once per connected piece.

### Cited Findings
- ESOLID: curve–curve intersection 54.2–98.2 % of total time across ten Bradley parts; within it resultants 3.6–71.1 % and Sturm sequences 1.9–94.3 %; an arbitrary-precision filter (PRECISE) cut the M16 rifle from 633 s to 43 s. — [Keyser 2002](http://gamma.cs.unc.edu/ESOLID/keyser02.pdf)
- Krishnan/BOOLE: patch-pair culling by AABB (control points, tightened by the trimming uv rectangle) with a Z-sweep and nested segment trees, O(n log² n + k) time but O(n² log² n) space; then Seidel LP separating-plane test O(k); only two ray shots per boolean thanks to component adjacency; a parallel implementation with a load-balancing algorithm (§7.3). — [Krishnan 1997](https://www.cs.unc.edu/xcms/wpfiles/dissertations/krishnan.pdf)
- HHK 1989: boxing all faces and intersecting the box set in O(n log² n + k) gives "performance close to linear" on the iterated-intersection sphere (12 intersections, final 3034 faces; last step 1085 s preprocessing + 6751 s on a 1987 Lisp machine). — [HHK 1989](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/cstech/624)
- Segal 1990: tolerant modeler "5–10 times slower, even when backtracking is not required"; O(n²) without spatial subdivision, O(n) average with it; backtracking worst case exponential but rare. — [Segal 1990](https://ftp1.cs.wisc.edu/pub/users/prem/segal-repair-siggraph-1990.pdf)
- OCCT: candidate pairs from bounding boxes; `SetUseOBB` (oriented boxes "can significantly speed-up the intersection stage" by reducing interfering pairs); `SetRunParallel`; Gluing "up to 90% in general, about 70% in the two examples"; Fuzzy 6e-5 on overlapping edges deviating by 5.28e-5: 45× speedup; `SetCheckInverted(false)` "up to 10 percent" on many solids; non-destructive mode "might slightly decrease the performance". — [OCCT spec](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/overview/html/specification__boolean_operations.html)
- **[Rank 14] Liang, S., Huang, H., Liang, H., Tang, W., Bao, A., Shen, E., Wang, J., Yang, Y., "Efficient Boolean operations for B-Rep models" (B-Rep 模型高效布尔运算算法), Journal of Computer-Aided Design & Computer Graphics 37(11):1993–, Nov 2025.** DOI https://doi.org/10.3724/SP.J.1089.2024-00367; PDF https://www.jcad.cn/en/article/pdf/preview/10.3724/SP.J.1089.2024-00367.pdf. Local: `.../Huang-2025-brep-boolean-efficiency-BVH-clustering.pdf` (Chinese with English abstract). "Leverages a Bounding Volume Hierarchy (BVH), face clustering, and local information near intersection edges to reduce the number of geometric intersection calculations. Additionally, techniques such as intersection graph construction and progressive imprinting (多次成环) are employed to enhance the algorithm's stability. Experimental evaluations using CAD models from real-world scenarios demonstrate that the proposed algorithm offers significant efficiency improvements over OCCT's Boolean operations and achieves performance comparable to that of Parasolid and ACIS in specific scenarios." Its related-work section notes Feito et al.'s octree face clustering reduces point-in-solid tests and parallelises ray–triangle tests, and that Nehring-Wirxel's BSP method is limited to convex cells. — [Liang/Huang 2025](https://www.jcad.cn/en/article/pdf/preview/10.3724/SP.J.1089.2024-00367.pdf)
- Zhou 2016: CGAL AABB-tree culling of triangle pairs; exact intersection "compute-bound" and parallelised with per-vertex mutexes despite CGAL lazy-exact reference counting; point location culled by component bounding boxes. — [Zhou 2016](http://www.cs.columbia.edu/cg/mesh-arrangements/mesh-arrangements-for-solid-geometry-siggraph-2016-zhou-et-al.pdf)
- Cherchi 2022: octree shared by intersection detection and ray casting; the most costly arrangement operations are "removal of duplicate and degenerate elements, the construction of adjacencies, the intersection computations and the final triangulation", all data-parallel with a work-stealing scheduler; patch construction and final extraction did not gain from fine-grained locking ("a meshlet-based approach is likely needed"); classification cost scales with patches not triangles; timings: 21.6 M triangles in 19.5 s vs 189.7 s (libigl). — [Cherchi 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.14151)
- Manifold: parallel BVH broad phase (Karras 2012); "the broad phase can take longer in practice than the intersection work itself"; triangulation and degenerate removal not yet parallel. — [Manifold wiki](https://github.com/elalish/manifold/wiki/Manifold-Library)
- Nehring-Wirxel 2021: ~2.5 M mesh-plane cuts per second single core; octree localisation keeps BSP merges at 100–200 nodes; per-cut cost grows sub-linearly with mesh size thanks to edge descent. — [Nehring-Wirxel 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.02486)

### Inferences
- cadrs is already 2–6× faster than OCCT on booleans (README), so the remaining performance levers from the literature are the same as the robustness levers: cull F-F pairs harder (OBB/BVH plus a control-polygon separating-plane test for B-spline faces), skip SSI for pairs whose contact is fully represented by common edges (Jackson/OCCT gluing), and classify once per connected piece. Parallelism in the published systems is over face pairs in the intersection stage and over patches in classification; the splitting/assembly stage is sequential everywhere.

### Gaps
- No published profile of a *tolerant curved* boolean (Parasolid/ACIS) is available; the only curved-B-rep timings found are ESOLID/BOOLE (exact vs float) and Liang/Huang 2025's comparison against Parasolid/ACIS/OCCT (numbers in the Chinese body text, not extracted here).
